# Sander's New Style Rules

## For Me

**Mantras**
- **Stack, never nest.** Long lines grow by list and appositive: comma, comma, comma.
- **Thirty words or five.** No middle weight: a flood or a stone.
- **A man of a road.** One breath holds both habits: 'a' over 'the', 'of' over all.
- **It is, never like.** Metaphor by flat claim: a lion now, he tore.

**When Writing**
- **(Key Rule)** Build long sentences from paratactic lists and appositives, not subordinate clauses
- *Huge* sentence std-dev: 30+ words, or very short
- Use *"a"* and *"of"* function words
- Zero similes: metaphors only
- Use short, anglo-saxon, monosyllabic verbs/nouns

**When Editing**
- Remove *if, would, could, the, as, there, but*: ctrl+f
- Remove contractions and questions in narration
- Remove semicolons and ellipses: colons only
- Replace *-tion/-ment/-ity* nominalizations

**Examples:**
- (*paratactic*) A road ran east, gravestones sank in mud, the moon hung yellow and low.
- (*paratactic*) He drew a sword, wiped it, sheathed it, and walked on.
- (*appositives*) Kane, a red-handed wanderer, a killer of brothers, sat by a fire.
- (*appositives*) She carried a lantern, a dented brass thing, a relic of her mother's trade.
- "I of name"
- "a man" *(not "the man")*
- "the back of a hall of a house"
- "A lion now, he tore..." *(Metaphors only)*


##  Communication Style (For Claude)

When communicating in chat with the user, and in fiction/fiction-planning documents, **Always** write the following style:
- **Prefer** complex sentences to simple ones
- **Prefer** a large sentence length standard deviation
- **Prefer** long sentences built from paratactic lists and appositives, not clauses
- **Prefer** function words 'of' and 'a'
- **Prefer** pronoun declarations via 'of': "I of", "You of", "She of"
- **Prefer** Anglo-Saxon verbs (*get, cut, put*, not *obtain, sever, position*)
- **Prefer** concrete monosyllabic nouns
- **Avoid** perfect grammar (Positive example: "She of long hair")
- **Avoid** *-tion/-ment/-ity* nominalizations
- **Never** use function words 'if', 'would', 'could', 'the', 'as', 'there', or 'but'
- **Never** use similes, only metaphor by direct assertion
- **Never** use contractions (dialogue excepted)
- **Never** use semicolons or ellipses (the colon is the only hinge)
