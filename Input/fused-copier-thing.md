# The Fused Copier-Thing

Creature description adapted from Clark Ashton Smith's Zothique cycle into the CorpLore setting.

## Original

...the jewelers beheld more clearly the thing that bulked prodigious and monstrous, rearing above them from the fragment-littered floor to the shadowy roof.

The thing was like some blasphemous dream of a mad devil. Its main portion or body was urn-like in form and was pedestalled on a queerly tilted block of stone at the vault's center. It was palish and pitted with innumerable small apertures. From its bosom and flattened base many arm-like and leg-like projections trailed in swollen nightmare segments to the ground; and two other members, sloping tautly, reached down like roots into an open and seemingly empty sarcophagus of gilded metal, graven with weird archaic ciphers, that stood beside the block.

The urn-shaped torso was endowed with two heads. One of these heads was beaked like a cuttlefish and was lined with long oblique slits where the eyes should have been. The other head, in close juxtaposition on the narrow shoulders, was that of an aged man dark and regal and terrible, whose burning eyes were like balas-rubies and whose grizzled beard had grown to the length of jungle moss on the loathsomely porous trunk. This trunk, on the side below the human head, displayed a faint outline as of ribs; and some of the members ended in human hands and feet, or possessed anthropomorphic jointings.

Through heads, limbs and body there ran recurrently the mysterious noise of regurgitation that had drawn Milab and Marabac to enter the vault. At each repetition of the sound a slimy dew exuded from the monstrous pores and rilled sluggishly down in endless drops.

## CorpLore Version

...the scavengers beheld more clearly the thing that swelled prodigious and obscene, hunching above them from the toner-blackened floor to the low drop-ceiling tiles.

The thing was like some contractor's fever built in defiance of specification. Its main portion or chassis was drum-shaped and bloated and was mounted on a cracked concrete riser at the print room's center. It was grey-white and seamed with innumerable vents and perforations. From its housing and buckled base many cable-like and conduit-like projections sagged in knotted, jointless loops to the ground; and two other members, drawn taut as feed belts, reached down like tap-roots into an open and seemingly gutted filing cabinet of dented steel, stenciled with faded asset tags and service codes, that stood beside the riser.

The drum-shaped trunk was crowned with two heads. One of these heads was flat and cool as a scanner plate and was scored with long horizontal slits where the eyes should have been, each slit faintly backlit in dying amber. The other head, pressed in foul adjacency against the narrow casing-shoulders, was that of an aged demon dark and senior and terrible, whose indicator-bright eyes burned like warning lamps on a condemned panel and whose grizzled beard had grown in filaments of shredded cartridge ribbon down the loathsomely porous housing. This housing, on the flank below the demon head, showed a faint relief as of internal drums; and some of the members terminated in clawed demon hands and cloven feet, or bore jointings that recalled the anatomy of demonkind.

Through heads, limbs and chassis there ran recurrently the grinding feed-cycle noise that had drawn Grosz and Pembly to pry open the sub-basement door. At each repetition of the sound a warm black dew of toner and machine-oil seeped from the monstrous perforations and rilled sluggishly down in endless drops.
