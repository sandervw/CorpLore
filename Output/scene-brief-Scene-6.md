# Scene Scene 6 Brief

## Premise

A demon pilgrim-knight, Igon, descends into a sealed sub-basement chasing the "haunting oboe hold-music" he heard as a child. The chamber houses an ancient sentient "fax-machine of intelligence" that has waited centuries to harvest the right kind of faith. As Igon hums his hold-song and fights deeper, the machine animates the Contractually Obligated dead against him in rising waves, capturing every sound he makes through bluetooth speaker-receivers clipped to the corpses. The cruel twist: the song Igon has been following is a false one, an artificial creation of the machine's own intranet, and every act of courage, every hum, has strengthened that false song which was only ever bait. By the end he has won every fight and lost everything.

## Scene Structure

- POV: third person limited, following Igon.
- Tense: past.
- Target wordcount: 500.
- Constraint: **Guilliam's monologue must take up at least 50% of the wordcount - IE. 50% of words delivered through direct speech**

## Scene Outline



- Igon stumbles into the fax-room. Description combining 'ancient' elements with 'corporate' ones:
  - Fax-machine sits on a bowed plastic table, against a stucco wall painted with neanderthal-like cave drawings.
  - Loose electric cords/wires spill from its front over the lip of the table (almost a lovecraftian look).
  - Two yellow-and-silver filing-cabinets (shrines) sit on either side of the table.
  - In the center of the floor is a circular rug depicting constellations from another sky; unfamiliar stars.
- Igon stands, swaying, victorious but completely drained.
- Igon does not reach the table. He walks halfway across the stone floor, over the middle of the carpet, but the despair in his heart drives him to the ground. He sits cross-legged, head bowed.
- Guilliam (the fax-machine) spends the remainder of the scene analyzing the newfound intelligence, not understanding that it can never come back to life, thinking it is on the verge of revival as its voice sings through the bluetooth speakers. He:
  - Dismisses Igon's faith in the oboe hold-music, claiming it was only a stolen piece of his own intranet.
  - Laments the loss of his most obligated employees (he thought the corpses were in fact alive), but reflects that more workers may be hired.
  - Probes Igon with questions about the world outside the sub-basement: how many of the old systems remain on the floors above?
  - Gloats that every sound Igon made, every word of optimism or phrase of song he cheered himself with, only strengthened Guilliam, and his false signal.
- Defining image: the knight sitting cross-legged, alone, panting in silence, a legion of skeletons in business suits scattered around him, with the half-alive, twisted fax machine between the two filing-cabinet shrines before him.
- Abrupt cut after Guilliam's final monologue. Ambiguous finale for Igon: does he live? What happens next?

## Carry-Forward State

Igon picks up bleeding from a forearm cut above the bracer and a low cut across the cuisse, drenched in cold sweat, exhausted from the conference-room battle. He is no longer humming; his own song has gone out. He fights with a heavy guillotine (a glass-bladed weapon). The machine speaks in a tinny synthetic music-voice through bluetooth speaker-receivers clipped to corpses and embedded in fire-alarm housings. It is named Guilliam. The false hold-music is still ringing/singing even after the last foe fell.

## Ending Text (Scene 5)

...The final foe was fragile, osseous, its bones like sponge. Igon clove it with a weary, almost idle swing. Silence dropped among the contractually obligated. Yet the music remained.
