# Mr. Sixpounder: Scene 1 - Synopsis

## Setting

The Eastmark Stairwells of Hotel Stackz, between the fourteenth and sixteenth landings. Time: approximately 2:47 AM. The stairwells rise in tight, identical spirals through the hotel, lit by humming fluorescent tubes. The carpet is ancient and decaying, worn bald in places by decades of foot traffic and supernatural patrol routes.

## Characters

- **Hindemith** - A veteran hotel staff member, meticulous and unflappable. He wears a charcoal suit re-stitched so many times the seams have become their own color. He manages crises. He is the scene's anchor: the man who has seen everything.
- **Kovac** - A new hire in Night Logistics, three days into her employment. She is restocking vending machines on East 14 when she encounters Mr. Sixpounder for the first time and is paralyzed with terror. She serves as an audience surrogate, her shock and disbelief giving Hindemith reason to explain how the hotel works.
- **Mr. Sixpounder** - A revenant and "Permanent Resident, subsection Non-Responsive" of Hotel Stackz. He patrols the Eastmark Stairwells nightly from 2:40 to 4:15 AM and has done so for decades. His chestplate has fused to his ribcage into a corroded hybrid texture categorized by maintenance as "structural." Carpet fibers have woven into his shins. Three of his fingers are original; the rest have been replaced by cutlery (a salad fork, a butter knife, something that may have been a corkscrew). He carries a sword. He does not speak or acknowledge people directly, but responds to proximity by turning his head toward the wall beside whoever is near him. His gait produces a distinctive lurching rhythm: "Hitch. Drop. Hitch."
- **The Tuple** - A Weave-Born entity that has risen from a bald patch in the carpet on the fourteenth landing. It resembles Dettmer from Accounts, but degraded, like a photocopy of a photocopy. Its name badge reads "DTTMR." Its tie is knotted in a configuration that doesn't exist. It stares at nothing with "vague administrative purpose" and does not interact with anyone.
- **Dettmer** - Referenced but not present. A demon from Accounts whose likeness the Tuple has copied.

## Plot Summary

At 2:47 AM, thirteen minutes before Mr. Sixpounder's patrol is due to pass through, a Tuple rises from an exposed patch of raw Weave in the carpet of the fourteenth landing. The Tuple is a degraded copy of Dettmer from Accounts and simply stands there, staring at nothing. Hindemith recognizes what it is and knows it won't leave on its own, but he has a more urgent problem: a new hire named Kovac hasn't checked in for forty minutes, and she's somewhere in the same stairwell that Mr. Sixpounder is about to patrol.

Hindemith leaves the Tuple and climbs upward. He finds Kovac on the sixteenth landing, flattened against the wall in terror. She has encountered Mr. Sixpounder descending on his nightly rounds and is barely coherent. Through their exchange, Hindemith calmly explains the revenant's nature: he is a permanent guest, decades-old, who patrols on a fixed schedule. The protocol is to stand still, speak evenly, and never offer him anything from the minibar (the last person who did became a "decorative installation" on the ninth landing).

Mr. Sixpounder then rounds the landing above them, and the scene gives its full description of the revenant: fused armor and bone, carpet-threaded shins, cutlery fingers clicking against a sword pommel. He passes within four feet of Kovac without acknowledging either of them, beyond a brief head turn toward the wall beside Hindemith.

After Sixpounder passes, Hindemith and Kovac descend together back to the fourteenth landing, where the Tuple is still standing motionless. Hindemith explains what it is and notes it will need to be filed. He hands Kovac a clipboard and pen that have been clipped to the railing for exactly this purpose, and delivers the scene's closing line.

## Key Worldbuilding Details

- **The Weave**: The structural grammar of reality, visible as a geometric pattern beneath worn carpet. When exposed, entities can use it as an entry point to manifest.
- **Tuples**: Weave-Born entities that emerge from exposed Weave. They take the form of degraded copies of existing people, with details "negotiated away" in the copying process.
- **Revenants / Contractually Obligated**: Undead permanent residents of the hotel. Mr. Sixpounder is classified under "Permanent Residents, subsection Non-Responsive" in the guest registry. His body decomposes at a controlled rate and smells of towel detergent (laundered on a dedicated housekeeping cycle) with an underlying copper note.
- **Bureaucratic Infrastructure**: The hotel has forms for everything, including Tuple manifestations. Maintenance categorizes anomalous biological-metal fusions as "structural." Replacement carpet requests are routinely misrouted across nine thousand stairwells. A clipboard is pre-positioned on the fourteenth-landing railing specifically for incidents like this.
- **The Minibar Incident**: A previous person offered Mr. Sixpounder something from the minibar and became fused into what is now called a "decorative installation" on the ninth landing. This is presented as cautionary institutional knowledge.

## Themes and Tone

The scene establishes the story's central tonal register: cosmic horror domesticated by bureaucracy. The terrifying (a fused-armor revenant patrolling with cutlery fingers and a sword) is rendered mundane through procedure, paperwork, and institutional familiarity. Hindemith embodies this tension. He is not brave so much as habituated. His cuff-adjusting is described as "load-bearing," a gesture that holds sentences together the way his re-stitched suit holds its shape. He is a man defined by maintenance rather than replacement, both of objects and of composure.

Kovac's role is to be the newcomer whose fear makes the audience feel the strangeness that Hindemith has long since filed away. The scene's comedy comes from the gap between her raw terror and his procedural calm.

The Tuple subplot introduces the Weave as a worldbuilding element and demonstrates that the hotel's problems are layered and simultaneous. Hindemith juggles multiple crises not because he's heroic, but because the hotel generates them faster than anyone can resolve them, and the forms keep coming regardless.
