# Mr. Sixpounder: Scene 1

The Tuple rose at 2:47, which was thirteen minutes before Mr. Sixpounder's patrol, and I will confess that the timing struck me as purposeful... not malicious, no, but purposeful in the way the hotel itself is purposeful, which is to say layered and simultaneous and very nearly beautiful if you stand at the right angle and do not flinch. The Weave had found a bald patch in the carpet on the fourteenth landing, the geometric grammar of it surfacing through the threadbare pile like light through stained glass, and out of that breach a Tuple had negotiated itself into being. It wore Dettmer's face. Or rather it wore what remained of Dettmer's face after the copying process had taken its toll, like a photocopy of a photocopy, the features softened into suggestion. Its name badge read DTTMR. Its tie was knotted in a configuration I have never seen in any manual, and I have read them all. It simply stood there, staring at nothing with what I can only describe as vague administrative purpose, and I thought: yes. Yes, of course. The Weave provides, even when what it provides is a degraded copy of a demon from Accounts who stares at walls.

I adjusted my cuffs. I do that. The gesture is load-bearing, I think, in the way my suit is load-bearing, re-stitched so many times the seams have become their own color, a third textile born from thread and repair. I am a demon defined by maintenance rather than replacement, and there is, I believe, a profound dignity in that. I glanced at the Tuple once more and let it be.

The more pressing matter was Kovac. She had not checked in for forty minutes, and she was mine to account for.

Kovac. Three days into Night Logistics, stocking vending machines on East 14, and now somewhere in the Eastmark Stairwells while Mr. Sixpounder began his descent. The stairwells rise in tight identical spirals through the hotel, lit by humming fluorescent tubes that have hummed so long I suspect they have developed opinions. The carpet up there is ancient, worn bald in places by decades of foot traffic and supernatural patrol routes. I climbed. I left the Tuple standing in its impossible tie and I climbed, because that is what one does. One climbs toward the problem. One does not wait for the problem to descend, particularly when the problem is a revenant with cutlery for fingers and a sword.

I found her on the sixteenth landing. Flattened against the wall. Her hands were pressed flat to the plaster and her breathing was the kind that comes in short bright bursts like someone trying to inflate something that will not hold air. She had seen him. Of course she had seen him. Three days is not enough orientation for Mr. Sixpounder. Three years might not be. But the hotel does not wait for readiness, friend, it simply generates crises faster than anyone can resolve them, and the forms keep coming regardless.

"Kovac," I said, and I kept my voice even, the way one keeps a flame steady in a draft. "Kovac, look at me. What you saw is a permanent guest."

She looked at me the way new hires always look at me when I say things like that. As though I had gone mad, or perhaps as though the madness was the world and I was simply the first demon to speak its language in her presence.

"He is classified under Permanent Residents, subsection Non-Responsive," I continued, adjusting my cuffs again because the sentence required structural support. "His name is Mr. Sixpounder. He has patrolled these stairwells nightly from 2:40 to 4:15 AM for decades. He will not speak to you. He will not acknowledge you. The protocol is to stand still, speak evenly, and never, under any circumstances, offer him anything from the minibar."

"The minibar," she whispered, as though the word itself was a test she might fail.

"The last demon who offered him a beverage became a decorative installation on the ninth landing. I have seen it. It is structural, now. Maintenance categorized it as such. They categorize most things as structural eventually, which I find oddly comforting..."

And then he came.

Hitch. Drop. Hitch.

That rhythm. I have heard it a thousand times and still my cuffs need adjusting when it rounds a corner. Mr. Sixpounder descended from the landing above us, and oh, friend, what can I tell you about him that the filing system has not already tried to contain? His chestplate has fused to his ribcage into a corroded hybrid texture that is neither armor nor bone but some third thing, oxidized and intimate. The hotel had claimed him thread by thread as well: carpet fibers woven into his shins from decades of the same patrol over the same threadbare route, claiming him the way it claims everything. Three of his fingers were original. The rest had been replaced by cutlery -- a salad fork, a butter knife, something that may once have been a corkscrew -- and they clicked against the pommel of his sword as he walked, a small percussive music like keys on a ring. He smelled of towel detergent, laundered on a dedicated housekeeping cycle, with an underlying copper note that I have learned not to investigate.

He passed within four feet of Kovac. She did not breathe. I do not think she could have. He did not look at her. He did not look at me. What he did was turn his head toward the wall beside me, which is what he always does, this sideways acknowledgment of proximity that is not recognition, not greeting, just... orientation. A compass needle swinging toward something that is not north. And then he was past, and the rhythm receded down the spiral. Hitch. Drop. Hitch. Growing softer. Growing structural.

I exhaled. I adjusted my cuffs. Kovac peeled herself from the wall in stages, like wallpaper remembering it was a demon.

"We need to go back down," I told her. "There is a Tuple on the fourteenth landing that needs filing."

She followed me. She did not speak, but she followed, and there is a kind of cooperation in that, a companionship of shared bewilderment that I have always found... warming. We descended together through the fluorescent hum and the ancient carpet and the fading copper smell, and on the fourteenth landing the Tuple was exactly where I had left it. Still wearing Dettmer's negotiated face. Still staring at nothing. Still knotted in its impossible tie.

I reached for the clipboard and pen that have been clipped to the railing for exactly this purpose. Someone, years ago, had the foresight to position them here, and I do not know who that demon was, but I believe they understood something essential about the hotel: that the forms must always be within reach, because the crises never stop, and the distance between horror and procedure is only ever the length of one arm extending toward a clipboard.

I handed it to Kovac. She took it. Her hands were still shaking, but she took it, and that was enough.

"Welcome to Hotel Stackz," I said. "Your orientation was incomplete."
