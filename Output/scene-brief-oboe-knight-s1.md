# Scene oboe-knight-s1 Brief

## Premise

A demon pilgrim-knight, Igon of Allsode, descends into a sealed sub-basement beneath the Westlake Companeye corporation, hunting a beautiful, swan-like oboe melody he once heard as a child on hold music and has obsessed over ever since. He does not know the chamber below holds Guilliam the Unthinking, an ancient fax-machine of intelligence that has waited centuries for someone of his exact faith to arrive. As Igon hums his way deeper, his song is harvested through bluetooth speakers clipped to the Contractually Obligated dead, animating them in escalating waves. He wins every fight and loses everything: the final chamber resounds with a tinny, synthetic version of his own song, manufactured by the machine he was searching for.

## Scene Structure

- POV: Third person limited, on Igon.
- Tense: Past.
- Target wordcount: ~500.
- Shape: Atmospheric descent. No combat, no dialogue partner. Igon alone, talking and humming to himself. End on him turning back into the chamber, no break to Scene 2.

## Character Voice / Speech Notes

Igon speaks aloud to himself in a warm, jovial tone, projected through his iron helm. Relentlessly optimistic. He hums an oboe melody, swan-like and haunting, but in his throat it is just a hum. He does not yet know the melody he hums and the melody he seeks are the same; do not let him recognize this. He refers to the corporation above as **Westlake Companeye**.

## Setting / Sensory Anchors

- Sloped entry, long and straight, descending.
- Rounded sandstone/stucco walls. This is a truly ancient office.
- Air thick with the pungent odor of flat, pressurized HVAC fumes.
- The descent terminates at a trapped sliding door of thick, fogged plexiglass.
- The door is sound-triggered (resonance door); humming opens it. Igon does not understand this; he believes he stumbled on the lock's secret by luck.
- The door closes harmlessly behind him once he is through.

## Foreshadowing to Seed (Downstream Setups)

- The working fax-machines of Westlake Companeye have, for centuries, left this sub-basement undefiled. Frame this as Igon's only piece of received knowledge about the place, and let the prose hint (without Igon catching it) that there is good reason for that avoidance.
- The plexiglass door reacting to his hum should land as fortunate to Igon but ominous to the reader. The mechanism that opened the door is the same mechanism that will later wake the dead.
- Do not name Guilliam, the fax-machine, the bluetooth speakers, or the oboe-as-target. Igon's specific purpose stays unstated this scene; it is revealed in Scene 3.

## Scene Outline

- Igon descends the sloped entry. Rounded sandstone/stucco walls, the air thick with pungent flat-pressurized HVAC fumes. A truly ancient office.
- He speaks to himself as he walks. The only thing he knows about this sub-basement: the working fax-machines of Westlake Companeye, the corporation built atop it, have for centuries left it undefiled. (Foreshadow: there is good reason. Do not state the reason.)
- He hums his song to himself as he goes.
- The long, straight descent ends at a trapped sliding door of thick, fogged plexiglass.
- Exposition by action:
  - Igon stops and presses his ear to the door. He hears nothing. He is discouraged.
  - He hums to himself, for motivation.
  - The plexiglass vibrates against his ear. He jumps back. The door slides open.
- Igon remarks on his good fortune. He assumes he accidentally stumbled on the secret that has kept the sub-basement locked.
- The door closes harmlessly behind him.
- He tests it: he hums near it again. It opens. Reassured, he turns back to face the chamber beyond.
- No break between this scene and the next.
