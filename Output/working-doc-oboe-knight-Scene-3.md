# oboe-knight: Scene 3 Working Doc

## Pre-Scene Work

### Scene Type

**Continuation.** Same sub-basement, same time, picking up mid-stride from Scene 2. No re-orienting the reader to the location.

### Purpose

**Audience:** Visceral combat with a back-and-forth arc; a tender beat where Igon voices his quest; unease that every sound he makes is being eaten by something unseen.

**Story:** First explicit statement of Igon's purpose. Plants Bluetooth-necromancy without naming it. Delivers Guilliam's long-awaited harvest. Sets up the sealed door and crumbled-wall workaround for Scene 4.

### Textures

1. Blue Glass Guillotine: weight in two hands, the chipped sea-green edge.
2. Brass clasps on sternums, speakers clipped over breastbone.
3. Server-glow: faint aqua bars pulsing on dead chassis, fan-blades still.
4. The dead's gait: slow orbital shuffle, heads canted toward Igon.
5. A patella skittering across stone tile; a humerus parting at the joint.
6. Stucco wall crumbled inward, rebar bent like grey reeds.

### Characters

1. **Igon:** Unhurt, energized, humming, helm-muffled jovial. Wants to quiet the dead by prayer, press deeper, and (now voiced aloud) find his childhood oboe hold-song.
2. **The Contractually Obligated (dead):** Slow orbit toward Igon. Want only to close on the sound.

### Location

T-section hallway in Westlake Companeye's sub-basement. Four plexiglass doors open onto four server rooms, one server each, fans silent, chassis lights faintly aqua. Stone tile, bone-grey stucco. A golden water cooler stands at the T. One threshold wears a cursed carpet. One far wall has crumbled to bent rebar and broken concrete.

## Draft

### Setup

The hallway opened at a T. A golden water cooler stood in the angle of it, sweating one slow bead down its flank, and beyond the cooler four slatted doors of white wood gave onto four server rooms.

"Ohh, what a lovely station," said Igon. "And I do believe the song is nearer in this place. The oboe. That swan-throated hold-song I hold most dear from my dreams."

At the first slatted door he stopped. A square of plum-and-bone carpet bristled before the threshold, its pile high, as if to welcome his step. Igon considered the carpet, declined the carpet, and stepped widely around it into the room.
