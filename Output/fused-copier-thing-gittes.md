# The Fused Copier-Thing

Creature description adapted from Clark Ashton Smith's Zothique cycle into the CorpLore setting. Gittes voice revision.

## Original

...the jewelers beheld more clearly the thing that bulked prodigious and monstrous, rearing above them from the fragment-littered floor to the shadowy roof.

The thing was like some blasphemous dream of a mad devil. Its main portion or body was urn-like in form and was pedestalled on a queerly tilted block of stone at the vault's center. It was palish and pitted with innumerable small apertures. From its bosom and flattened base many arm-like and leg-like projections trailed in swollen nightmare segments to the ground; and two other members, sloping tautly, reached down like roots into an open and seemingly empty sarcophagus of gilded metal, graven with weird archaic ciphers, that stood beside the block.

The urn-shaped torso was endowed with two heads. One of these heads was beaked like a cuttlefish and was lined with long oblique slits where the eyes should have been. The other head, in close juxtaposition on the narrow shoulders, was that of an aged man dark and regal and terrible, whose burning eyes were like balas-rubies and whose grizzled beard had grown to the length of jungle moss on the loathsomely porous trunk. This trunk, on the side below the human head, displayed a faint outline as of ribs; and some of the members ended in human hands and feet, or possessed anthropomorphic jointings.

Through heads, limbs and body there ran recurrently the mysterious noise of regurgitation that had drawn Milab and Marabac to enter the vault. At each repetition of the sound a slimy dew exuded from the monstrous pores and rilled sluggishly down in endless drops.

## CorpLore Version (Gittes Voice)

...so Grosz and I got the sub-basement door open. The grinding had been going on a while, the kind of noise you hear once and spend the rest of your career pretending you didn't. Grosz had to look, and I had to look because Grosz was looking.

The thing filled the print room from the toner-blackened floor to the drop-ceiling tiles. Drum-shaped, bloated, grey-white, mounted on a cracked concrete riser like somebody had poured a foundation for it. The chassis was seamed with vents and perforations, hundreds of them, and I stopped counting because counting wasn't helping. From the buckled base, cable-thick projections sagged in knotted, jointless loops to the ground, like something that had forgotten how limbs work. Two other members ran taut as feed belts into a gutted filing cabinet beside the riser. Dented steel. Faded asset tags. Service codes nobody had read in years. Somebody built this, or fed it. I wanted to know who.

It had two heads. Stay with me.

One was flat and cool as a scanner plate, scored with horizontal slits where you'd expect eyes, each slit backlit in dying amber, the kind of light that means a machine hasn't decided whether to stay on. The other head, pressed against the narrow casing-shoulders in a way that made my stomach turn, was an aged demon's. Dark, senior, terrible. Eyes like warning lamps on a condemned panel. Beard grown in filaments of shredded cartridge ribbon down the porous housing. Below that head the housing showed a faint relief of internal drums. Some of the members ended in clawed demon hands, some in cloven feet, some in jointings that recalled proper anatomy, which made it worse.

Through the whole apparatus the feed-cycle kept grinding. Each time it cycled, warm black dew of toner and machine-oil seeped from the perforations and crawled down in endless drops. That was the sound that brought us here. I was going to be hearing it a long time.
