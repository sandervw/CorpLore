# Scene Frame — Scene 4

## Scene Parameters

**Senses:** smell, temperature, pain
The passage carries no visual relief; foreground sensation is the cold sweat soaking Igon's shirt, the tomb-dust sting in his eyes, and the dull throb of battered muscles — smell, temperature, and pain anchor every beat.

**Rhythms:** paratactic / balanced
Paratactic suits the mechanical plod of descent (one corridor, then another, then another); balanced rhythm for the moment of realization, when two things — the machine's mimicry and his own song — are held in opposition.

**Scene shape:** deflation
The scene begins with hard-won momentum and ends with the lock broken but the knight diminished; faith has leaked out with each step down.

**Color palette:** violets-and-yellows
Single yellow bulbs on chains; violet shadow filling everything else. Stain-of-bruise coloring doubles as the palette of doubt encroaching on optimism.

## Source Imprint

1. **Sentence-length distribution:** alternates very short declaratives (3-7 words) with medium periodic sentences (18-30 words); almost no middle ground.
2. **Clause stacking:** right-branching, additive; new information appended with commas, rarely subordinated.
3. **Register:** plain-modern with occasional elevated vocabulary ("unconscionable," "suffused," "adherents"); contractions absent from narration and rare in speech.
4. **Dominant sensory channels:** sight and sound dominate; texture appears once ("glistening," "black transparent jelly").
5. **Signature tics:** declarative short sentences used as scene pivots ("The cylinder was alive."); direct speech reported with minimal tags.
6. **Punctuation habit:** dashes and exclamation marks used in speech for excitement; narration stays restrained.

**Do:** Drop declarative pivots as scene-beat anchors. Let short sentences land alone. Append detail rightward, not in nested clauses. Keep narration plain; let one Latinate word per paragraph do the elevation.

**Don't:** Stack subordinate clauses inside one another. Use contractions in narration. Let sensory catalogues run past two items before a new sentence begins.

## Speech Rules

### Igon

- **Positive mode:** Warm exclamatory opener ("Ohh," / "Aha!") + greeting; self-deprecating "Hah!" after grandiosity or awkwardness.
- **Register:** Slightly formal, old-fashioned, courtly-but-conversational. Sparing contractions.
- **Sentence-length cap:** 20 words. Short exclamations and ellipsis-trailed fragments allowed at emotional peaks.
- **Signature construction:** Requests framed as invitations / rhetorical questions.
- **Default-banned:** Commands or imperatives; modern slang; cynicism; mockery aimed outward.

## Forbidden Patterns

- no similes whose vehicle is a tradesman's craft
- no `like a` constructions
- no isolated fragment sentences
- no exclusively muted/grey color palette
- no description of Igon's realization rendered as an interior monologue summary; show the inference through physical reaction and broken rhythm
- no oboe-sound described via musical metaphor (e.g., "a note like a lament")
- no optimism staged as a rallying speech to himself; doubt must be visible in what he does
- no plexiglass-shattering moment described in slow-motion cinematic detail
