# Scene Scene 5 Brief

## Premise

Igon, a demon pilgrim-knight, has descended into a sealed sub-basement beneath Westlake Companeye in search of a haunting oboe song he heard as a child on hold music. The chambers hold the Contractually Obligated dead, animated by an ancient fragment of intelligence (the fax-machine Guilliam) via small bluetooth speakers clipped to corpses and embedded in fire alarms. The fax-machine has waited centuries for someone carrying exactly Igon's song so it can harvest it. Every note Igon hums, and every sound of his combat, is being captured and fed to Guilliam through those same speakers. Igon does not yet realize this; he believes his courage and his song are weapons against the thing below.

## Scene Structure

- **POV:** Third-person limited on Igon
- **Tense:** Past
- **Target wordcount:** 800

**At least 70% of words MUST BE devoted to action**

## Already Rendered (DO NOT RE-DESCRIBE)

- Igon's armor, helm, guillotine, and gait (established in earlier scenes).
- The synthetic, tinny "false hold music" tracking just behind Igon's hummed melody (now a constant under-note).
- The clipped-walnut bluetooth speakers and their role animating the dead.
- Tomb-grit on visor and eye-rim; sweat clinging clammily between shoulder blade and breastplate; salt sting at the eye.

## Carry-Forward State

- Igon has just smashed through the warded plexiglass with his guillotine and stepped over the shards. He enters this scene already breathing hard, sweat-slick, eyes stinging, the false note still threaded behind his ear.
- He now understands the bluetooth-speaker mechanism but has not yet realized the speakers are *recording* him, not just broadcasting at him.

## Upstream Revelations (active in this scene)

- The speakers animate the dead; destroy the speaker and the corpse falls.
- The "false oboe" tracks Igon's own humming, half a hair off pitch.
- Igon does *not* know the song is being harvested; the audience should feel the trap closing while Igon fights on in good faith.

## Downstream Setups

- By scene's end, Igon should be visibly, completely drained, the music in the chamber should be louder and fuller than ever (closer to the true dream-song), and Igon's own humming should have gone quie. This sets the silent, defeated tableau of Scene 6.
- Guilliam's voice through the speakers should be probing, curious, asking about the floors above and the outer world. He does not threaten; he interviews.

## Setting anchors

- Large conference room. Single ebony table at its center.
- The Contractually Obligated dead here wear fine, jewel-sewn business-formal suits of a precious silver color, and carry enterprise-grade steel swords. They are *already* animated and armed when Igon enters.
- The false hold-music swells louder through speakers on corpses and (implied) in the walls as more dead fall and more sound is harvested.

## Scene Outline

**The battle must take up at least 70% of the wordcount - IE. at least 70% of words MUST BE devoted to the action**

- Igon enters a large conference room with a single ebony table.
- The dead here are dressed in fine, jewel-sewn business-formal suits of a precious silver color.
- As Igon enters, the Contractually Obligated dead are already awake; all of them are armed with enterprise-grade steel swords.
- The dead turn on Igon, and the final, great battle takes place.
  - Extended, *specific* description of violence; describe the carnage; Igon battering/smashing through corpse after corpse, while the fax machine only sings louder and louder through its animated dead.
  - *Excruciating detail:* what specific maneuvers does Igon use, what are the specific movements for Igon and the risen dead, how many are their number, how are their bodies affected?
  - *Back-and-forth combat:* the fight should have a back/forth, ups and downs — it should capture a mini-story-arc across the combat.
- All the while during the combat, the fax-machine's voice speaks, taunting, studying, asking questions about the outside world.
  - Louder and louder rings the knight's stolen hold music, through the bluetooth speaker-receivers.
- The scene cuts on Igon finally striking the last decisive blow.
