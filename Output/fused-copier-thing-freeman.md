# The Fused Copier-Thing (Freeman Voice)

Creature description adapted from Clark Ashton Smith's Zothique cycle into the CorpLore setting. Revised in the Freeman voice.

## Original

...the jewelers beheld more clearly the thing that bulked prodigious and monstrous, rearing above them from the fragment-littered floor to the shadowy roof.

The thing was like some blasphemous dream of a mad devil. Its main portion or body was urn-like in form and was pedestalled on a queerly tilted block of stone at the vault's center. It was palish and pitted with innumerable small apertures. From its bosom and flattened base many arm-like and leg-like projections trailed in swollen nightmare segments to the ground; and two other members, sloping tautly, reached down like roots into an open and seemingly empty sarcophagus of gilded metal, graven with weird archaic ciphers, that stood beside the block.

The urn-shaped torso was endowed with two heads. One of these heads was beaked like a cuttlefish and was lined with long oblique slits where the eyes should have been. The other head, in close juxtaposition on the narrow shoulders, was that of an aged man dark and regal and terrible, whose burning eyes were like balas-rubies and whose grizzled beard had grown to the length of jungle moss on the loathsomely porous trunk. This trunk, on the side below the human head, displayed a faint outline as of ribs; and some of the members ended in human hands and feet, or possessed anthropomorphic jointings.

Through heads, limbs and body there ran recurrently the mysterious noise of regurgitation that had drawn Milab and Marabac to enter the vault. At each repetition of the sound a slimy dew exuded from the monstrous pores and rilled sluggishly down in endless drops.

## CorpLore Version (Freeman Voice)

...so Pembly finally gets the sub-basement door open and we can see this thing properly and it's just -- okay. Okay. It goes from the floor, which is black with toner, all the way up to the drop-ceiling tiles, and I'm already thinking: we should leave. We should absolutely leave.

The best way I can describe it is someone built a copier to no specification whatsoever and then it kept going. The main body is this bloated drum shape, grey-white, mounted on a cracked concrete riser in the center of the print room -- and the whole surface is just vents and perforations, like a massive industrial cheese grater that hates you. Cables and conduit-looking projections are hanging off the housing in these knotted loops, dragging on the ground, and two other pieces are pulled taut like feed belts going straight down into a gutted filing cabinet. Dented steel, faded asset tags, the whole thing -- it's feeding on a filing cabinet. That's great. That's exactly what I needed to see today.

It has two heads. One is flat like a scanner plate, scored with horizontal slits where eyes should be, each one barely glowing this sickly amber -- like dying indicator lights, which, by the way, means it's still on. The other head is a demon's, old and terrible, crammed right up against the first on these narrow casing-shoulders. Its eyes burn like warning lamps on a panel that's already been condemned, and its beard is made of shredded cartridge ribbon growing down the housing, which is porous and disgusting. Below the demon head you can see the outline of internal drums through the casing, and some of the cable-arms end in actual clawed demon hands and cloven feet, which -- I don't even know what to do with that information.

And the whole time, through the heads, the limbs, the chassis, there's this grinding feed-cycle noise -- that's what made us pry the door open in the first place, by the way, thank you Pembly -- and every time it cycles, warm black toner and machine-oil just seeps out of every perforation and drips down the sides. Endlessly. I can smell it from here and it is not a workplace-safe concentration of anything.
