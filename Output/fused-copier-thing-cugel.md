# The Fused Copier-Thing (Cugel Voice)

Creature description adapted from Clark Ashton Smith's Zothique cycle into the CorpLore setting, revised in the voice of Cugel the Clever.

## Cugel Voice

...and so, with Grosz wheezing at my heels, I shouldered open the sub-basement door and there beheld the thing entire. It hunched from the toner-blackened floor to the sagging drop-ceiling tiles, prodigious, obscene, and, to my practiced eye, entirely without resale value.

A lesser demon would have fled. The thing's main chassis was drum-shaped and bloated, mounted on a cracked concrete riser at the center of what had once been a print room of some consequence. The housing was grey-white, seamed with innumerable vents and perforations, each oozing toner-slurry of the lowest possible grade. From its buckled base, cable-like and conduit-like projections sagged in knotted loops to the ground -- and here I paused to consider whether the cabling might fetch three terces per coil, were one to strip and bundle it. Two other members, drawn taut as feed belts, reached like tap-roots into a gutted filing cabinet of dented steel, stenciled with faded asset tags. The cabinet itself: worthless.

The trunk was crowned with two heads, which struck me as excessive. One was flat and cool as a scanner plate, scored with horizontal slits faintly backlit in dying amber. The other, pressed in foul adjacency against the casing-shoulders, was that of an aged demon dark and senior and terrible, whose indicator-bright eyes burned like warning lamps on a condemned panel and whose grizzled beard had grown in filaments of shredded cartridge ribbon down the loathsomely porous housing. Below this head the casing showed a faint relief of internal drums, and some of the members terminated in clawed demon hands and cloven feet. I made a mental note to avoid these.

Through the whole assemblage ran a grinding feed-cycle noise -- the very sound that had drawn us here. At each repetition a warm black dew of toner and machine-oil seeped from the monstrous perforations and rilled sluggishly down in endless drops. I directed Grosz to investigate more closely, and withdrew to a prudent distance.
