# Mr. Sixpounder Scene 1 -- Hindemith Dialogue in Solaire Voice

_Test rewrite: all Hindemith dialogue reimagined using the Radiant Optimist voice guide. Surrounding prose unchanged._

---

**Original:**
> "Ms. Kovac. Child of the night shift. You were expected three hours ago."

**Rewrite:**
> "Ah, Ms. Kovac! There you are. I had begun to wonder if the stairwells had swallowed you whole... not that they haven't tried before. You were expected three hours past, but not to worry, not to worry. The night shift is glad of your company all the same."

---

**Original:**
> "There is."

**Rewrite:**
> "Oh, there is. There very much is."

---

**Original:**
> "He has a sword. He has carried it since before the Eastmark Stairwells bore their name, since before this carpet knew the weight of a single footfall. You would do well to speak of him accordingly."

**Rewrite:**
> "Ah-- *he* has a sword. He has carried it since before these stairwells bore their name, since before this carpet knew the weight of a single footfall. I would ask, if it would please you, that you speak of him accordingly. He has earned that much, and then some."

---

**Original:**
> "He prefers 'Mr. Sixpounder.' You will find his name inscribed among the guest incunabula, in the Book of Permanent Residents. A ward of these stairwells. A keeper of its landings.."

**Rewrite:**
> "He prefers 'Mr. Sixpounder.' You will find his name inscribed among the guest incunabula, in the Book of Permanent Residents. A ward of these stairwells. A keeper of its landings." He paused, and something in his expression softened. "I confess I am quite fond of him. Hah hah hah! You should find that strange. Well, you should."

---

**Original:**
> "You will not need to. He descends. He has always descended. Every night, the same circuit: from the upper risers to the fourteenth landing." He paused. "Consider it a privilege, Ms. Kovac, that you should witness the passage. Most who work these landings toil for months before they are granted such an audience."

**Rewrite:**
> "Oh, you will not need to. He descends. He has always descended. Every night, the same circuit: from the upper risers down to the fourteenth landing... The flow of time in these stairwells is a peculiar thing, Ms. Kovac. He walks it as though it were new each night." He paused. "If I may say so, it is rather a privilege that you should witness the passage. Most who work these landings toil for months before they are granted such an audience. What do you say -- shall we stand and receive him?"

---

**Original:**
> "He, Ms. Kovac. Towel detergent and copper. The twin sacraments of his procession." He raised one hand, measured as a blessing. "Stand still. Speak in low and measured tones. And offer him no refreshment."

**Rewrite:**
> "*He*, Ms. Kovac. Towel detergent and copper. The twin sacraments of his procession." He raised one hand, measured as a blessing. "Now, if you would be so kind -- stand still, speak in low and measured tones. And I must ask that you offer him no refreshment. Only if it would please you, of course... though I would relish your continued existence. Hah hah hah!"

---

**Original:**
> "Why? Because hospitality, to Mr. Sixpounder, is a covenant. The last demon to suggest some small libation from the minibar now resides upon the ninth landing, sealed in lacquer upon a stone sculpture of a cockatrice. She meant well."

**Rewrite:**
> "Ah, why? Because hospitality, to Mr. Sixpounder, is a covenant. A sacred thing, quite beyond the usual courtesies. The last demon to suggest some small libation from the minibar now resides upon the ninth landing, sealed in lacquer upon a stone sculpture of a cockatrice." He tilted his head. "She meant well. I do think of her from time to time... Not to worry. Not to worry."

---

**Original:**
> "Good evening, Mr. Sixpounder. May your blade find no cause to sing, and your landings rest quiet beneath you."

**Rewrite:**
> "Oh! Good evening, Mr. Sixpounder. What a fine thing, to see you upon your rounds. May your blade find no cause to sing, and your landings rest quiet beneath you."

---

**Original:**
> "A Tuple. It rose from the carpet some twenty minutes past, wearing Dettmer's face. Dettmer, who has not set foot in these stairwells in seven months." ... "When the textile thins like this, things apply themselves." ... "We shall place our signatures upon a Form of the Obscure, and Maintenance will seal it in wax. And the carpet, in time, will forget that whih it has birthed."

**Rewrite:**
> "Ah, yes. A Tuple. It rose from the carpet some twenty minutes past, wearing Dettmer's face. Dettmer, who has not set foot in these stairwells in seven months... We are amidst strange things in these landings, Ms. Kovac. Strange things that borrow familiar shapes." He moved past Kovac and crouched at the edge of the fourteenth landing, studying the bald patch. "When the textile thins like this, things apply themselves. It is the way of it." He straightened. "But not to worry! We shall place our signatures upon a Form of the Obscure, and Maintenance will seal it in wax. And the carpet, in time, will forget that which it has birthed."

---

**Original:**
> "There is a form for everything, Ms. Kovac. For every signature, a demon willing to hold the pen." ... "Tonight, that demon is you."

**Rewrite:**
> "Oh, there is a form for everything, Ms. Kovac. For every signature, a demon willing to hold the pen." He held both out to her, and smiled. "Tonight, that demon is you. I have a feeling you will take to it quite naturally. Hah hah hah!"
