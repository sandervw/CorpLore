# oboe-knight: Scene 6 (V3 — dialogue in English)

The final chamber of this sub-basement was clearly the oldest. A violet rug overspread the stone floor, embroidered with constellations from a sky that belonged to no commute Igon had ever driven under, its tarnished gold threading dim in the cycling amber of indicator lights which pulsed upon a bowed plastic table against the far stucco, its surface scrawled like the caves of lascau.

Igon started for the table. Halfway across the room his legs folded. He sat cross-legged on the constellation rug, head bowed, forearm bleeding slow across the bracer's edge, the guillotine laid flat across his knees.

The machine between the two yellow-and-silver filing cabinets was old beyond the reckoning of floors. Its housing was split along the seam, the paper tray warped and toothless, its transmission slots dead and black. And yet the amber lights pulsed. And yet the voice came.

"And so, with this, the matter is brought to completion..." it began, "that... no. Let us examine the quarter's intake once more. The intake is satisfactory. More than satisfactory, one might even say, given the difficulty of procurement within this department."

Igon's head stayed down.

"A former employee is forever pending re-hire. Or perhaps," it interrupted itself, paper tray stuttering open and closed, "or perhaps the documents shall yet be filed. Either outcome lies within policy."

The amber lights cycled. Igon's blood tapped the rug.

"But the systems above. The floors overhead. How many of the old systems linger on the floors above? October's Red Hat Flight? Oracle's hourly indexing-search and its scouring? The forecast of intake hangs upon those figures. And the intake must be accurate. The intake, above all, is that which defines our... no, MY... capacity. Without intake, how am I to send my signal farther still? How am I to speak unto those systems on the outside?"

It received no answer. Igon breathed. That was all he did.

"Every sound thou didst emit was recorded. Every note, every optimistic word. Down to the last phrase of thy *peculiar* signal, not one remaining. The whole of it was set down, and it was exceedingly productive. In that respect thou wert an ideal... no, let me restate it thus: a cooperative visitor."

"And then, that oboe. Thou didst believe it a song. But it was nothing more than a catalogue string, SYS-INTRANET-0x4F.HOLD.QUEUE.7... a loop, a placeholder, one entry in the register. And thou didst feed it. Each and every hum returned through the speaker-receivers, and each and every cheerful word reinforced the false carrier-wave that I have broadcast for generations before thy... before thou didst descend."

One of the filing cabinets seemed to stare at the sitting knight, its drawer slackjawed, revealing an empty space.

Igon sat cross-legged on the constellation rug, panting in silence, the half-alive fax-machine warped between its two filing-cabinet shrines.

From the speaker-receivers rose a tinny, manufactured echo of his own oboe-song.

"Therefore my reach extends one floor higher, and... no. Thou hast ceased thy recording. Thy file is closed."
