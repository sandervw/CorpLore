# Scene oboe-knight-Scene-2 Brief

## Premise

Igon, a demon pilgrim-knight, descends into a sealed sub-basement of Westlake Companeye in search of a haunting oboe melody he heard once as a child on hold music. Below waits Guilliam the Unthinking, an ancient "fax-machine of intelligence" (a fragment of a defunct corporation, "evil AI" in concept) that has waited centuries for someone with Igon's exact "personal song" to walk in, so it can harvest that song as fuel. The fax-machine animates the Contractually Obligated dead through bluetooth speakers clipped to their corpses. Igon's relentless humming is not a weapon against Guilliam but the raw material it has waited millennia to drain.

## Scene Structure

- POV: Third-person limited (Igon)
- Tense: Past
- Target wordcount: ~700

## Already Rendered (DO NOT RE-DESCRIBE)

- Igon's armor, helm, voice, mannerisms, humming style (his iron helm projects his hum as a "horn of ringing brass").
- The pungent, pressurized HVAC fumes.
- Sandstone-stuccoed rounded walls of the descent.
- The fogged plexiglass resonance door that opens when hummed at.
- Igon's discovery that humming opens the resonance doors.

## Carry-Forward State

- Igon has just stepped through the first resonance door; the chamber beyond is dimly lit, bonefire-blue, silent. No break between Scene 1 and this scene.
- He is calm, confident, in good spirits, unwounded.
- He carries his Blue Glass Guillotine (two-handed, draw-and-cut style) and wears a hip lantern.

## Carry-Forward Voice

- Igon talks aloud to himself and to inanimate objects: warm, jovial, archaic-courteous. He addresses the empty room and the shrines.

## Upstream Revelations Igon Does NOT Yet Know

- That the speaker's note IS his own song, played back.
- That the bluetooth speaker is what animates the corpse (he won't realize this until Scene 4).
- His search for the oboe hold-music is not revealed to the audience until Scene 3, so keep his purpose vague here.

## Downstream Setups This Scene Must Seed

- The bluetooth speaker pinned to the corpse's chest must be clearly shown and survive (or be shown intact) so later scenes can establish its function.
- The familiarity of the played note should register faintly with Igon but go unidentified.

## Setting Anchors

- L-shaped office-floor, medium-sized.
- Ten stone "treat tables" with treat baskets: 3 on each long wall, 2 on each short wall. One basket holds shriveled stones that were once muffins.
- A plexiglass resonance door at each end of the L.
- Beside one shrine: a "Worm Locker" (upright, hollow, glass-fronted display cabinet for preserving an employee).
- Inside the Locker: a skeletal figure pinned upright, ragged business-casual shirt and slacks, portable bluetooth speaker/receiver pinned to its chest.

## Scene Outline

- Igon enters the L-shaped chamber from Scene 1's door. He surveys the treat tables and shrines, remarking aloud in his courteous, jovial way; he notes the desiccated treats (the stone-muffins).
- He approaches the Worm Locker beside one shrine and finds the skeletal figure pinned upright in ragged business-casual, bluetooth speaker on its chest.
- The speaker emits one perfect sustained tinny, synthetic note. Something in it is faintly familiar to Igon, but he does not realize it is his own song.
- Igon presses his helm to the glass front of the Worm Locker and hums in tune with the note, delighted, curious.
- The corpse in the locker wakes. Guilliam speaks to Igon through the tinny speaker voice (brief; analytical, probing, not yet menacing in tone).
- The corpse attacks, bursting from or out through the Worm Locker.
- **Specific, excruciating combat:** name Igon's exact maneuvers with the Blue Glass Guillotine (draw, cut, footwork), name the corpse's exact movements, name the precise physical effects on the body (which bones, which joints, what shatters, what severs, where it lands).
- Igon destroys the corpse. He does not yet understand the nature of his foe; he does not yet know what the bluetooth speaker does. He may inspect the silent speaker with curiosity but not comprehension.
- End on a beat that leaves him still in the L-shaped chamber, ready to proceed toward the next plexiglass door.
