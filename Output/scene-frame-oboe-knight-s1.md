# Scene Frame — oboe-knight-s1

## Scene Parameters

**Senses:** smell, sight, proprioception

Smell anchors the descent (flat HVAC fumes, pressurized and ancient); sight tracks the geometry of the corridor and the fogged plexiglass door; proprioception carries the reader through the downward slope in Igon's body — the gradient in his joints, the resistance of the door against his ear.

**Rhythms:** periodic, long-coordinated

Periodic rhythm fits the slow, ceremonial descent — sentences that build toward revelation. Long-coordinated chains mimic Igon's unhurried, optimistic forward motion, clause after clause.

**Scene shape:** circular

Igon descends, tests the door, passes through, then turns back to test it again before facing the chamber — the return-and-retest motion closes a loop and grounds the transition.

**Color palette:** bonefire-blue-and-pitch-black

Cold darkness of the sub-basement broken by the faint blue-white of the fogged plexiglass; the warm, fleshy tone of Igon is the only heat source. Reinforces the ancient, sealed quality of the space.

## Source Imprint

**Observed features:**

- **Sentence-length distribution:** Short declaratives (2-6 words) cut against long periodic sweeps (20-35 words); the middle register is nearly absent.
- **Clause stacking:** Right-branching chains strung with semicolons and serial commas; occasional periodic suspension
- **Register:** Archaic-formal; Latinate vocabulary in motion verbs and spatial description; no contractions.
- **Dominant senses:** Sight and hearing in strict alternation; tactile only at threshold moments.
- **Signature tics:** Repetition as incantation ("back, forth, back, forth, down, down, down"); "they" subject sustains collective tension; staccato imperative ("Listen.") as beat-marker.

**Do:** Alternate short declaratives with long right-branching chains. Use repetition for descent and spatial movement. Let hearing arrive as a sudden intrusion into visual/olfactory scene.

**Don't:** Write mid-length sentences as the default. Avoid contractions. Do not explain sound; report it, then let the character react.

---

## Speech Rules

### Igon

- **Positive mode:** Warm exclamatory opener ("Ohh," / "Aha!") + greeting; self-deprecating "Hah!" after grandiosity or awkwardness.
- **Register:** Slightly formal, old-fashioned, courtly-but-conversational. Sparing contractions.
- **Sentence-length cap:** 20 words. Short exclamations and ellipsis-trailed fragments allowed at emotional peaks.
- **Signature construction:** Requests framed as invitations / rhetorical questions.
- **Default-banned:** Commands or imperatives; modern slang; cynicism; mockery aimed outward.

---

## Forbidden Patterns

**Rolled bans:**

- No similes whose vehicle is a tool or weapon.
- No `like a` constructions.
- No `not X, but Y` constructions.
- No isolated fragment sentences.
- No character described by what their hands are doing.
- No eyes described as reflective surfaces (mirrors, water, glass).
- No sententious closing lines (one-line moralisms).

**Scene-specific bans (PREDICTIVE):**

- Do not let Igon consciously recognize the connection between the melody he hums and the melody he seeks
- Do not name or describe Guilliam, the bluetooth speakers, or the oboe as a target; all machinery in the chamber beyond the door remains unnamed and unrecognized.
- Do not explain the resonance-door mechanism; present cause (hum) and effect (vibration, opening) without linking them analytically in narration or Igon's internal voice.
- Do not editorialize on why the fax-machines of Westlake Companeye avoided this sub-basement; foreshadow by tone and implication only.
