## Scene 1 - The Empty Office

**Arrival:**

Floor 253 keeps its tillage on the western side, along Cookie Boulevard, where the windows run a hundred feet high and a thousand wide - dense crystal, staring out and down over layer upon layer of the wooden platforms that scale the flanks of Mighty Obelisk. The windows throw rainbows on the sere and worn carpet running between a timeless elevator and an altar of marble, whereat a silver chalice of last-century's coffee has established its minor cult. I have never paid the chalice tribute.

The tillagers of this department - twenty-eight families, each with a two-or-three-bed cubicle of rustic brick fronting the glass - stand out from Obelisk's other departments of tillage by a practice they call 'voluntary taxation.' Each morning, back from harvest at the terrace gardens, every family sheds a portion of its millet and mushrooms into a large bowl of blue plastic, and this they carry to floor 253's Mulcher unit.

I was passing the front opening of the third cubicle as they gathered for it, and speaking to myself in no indoor voice, "Of all the sheepiest things I've seen of the demon workers of Obelisk, this dinky ceremony must-"

I halted, not from tact. My fine eyes had scanned ahead to the fourth cubicle, the only one-bed on Cookie Boulevard; and over its brick half-wall, where Perli ought to have straddled her office chair, upholstered in violet velvet, yet the chair was empty.

**Discovery:**

*In Arrears.*

I withheld my sight from the two words. They were written in indigo crayon on the crystal window directly opposite Perli's cubicle, fine and neat in a way no child's wall graffiti ever managed. I had needed only the glimpse. If the smell still hanging in the air - fresh-mown field grass over something like skunk spray, or dung - had not announced Danger in the words, the handwriting would have sufficed. I knew it from a 175-year-old treatise of astrology, *Vampires of the White Pinhole Sky*, and I knew the storied demon it belonged to.

"Ostrabawgewlus," I said, and laved the breadth of my chin. "It seems you've stolen my Perli. If she is jailed now in your black and stinking domain, I'll-"

**Parley:**

"Stop," a voice barked. "Those Mighty Words hold a magic obscure, and I've no wish to be eavesdropped by the demon you named."

I turned my sight back to Perli's cubicle, which had grown two mulcher deputies while I was busy addressing the window. The barker, M. Brayve, held a hand over his eyes and spoke to his fellow out of the corner of his mouth. "How do you suppose it's forged?"

"Forged?" replied Mulcher Perpull - a head shorter than Brayve, two shorter than yours truly, Big Gneiss Rong, and forty thin pounds lighter. "What's forged?"

"The indigo crayon. It's a powerful wax."

Perpull, not so versed in the occult as his better, or as me, turned and stared directly at the fine words, almost invisible against the shadowed blue dawn of Obelisk's western face. He scratched a freckle. "Maybe- Suppose he found some kind of 'waxworks' of replicas. There'd be plenty of colors to pick from."

"Hmm. What about the crayon's shape."

**Argument:**

I marched through the cubicle opening and stood before the two of them. "What matters is the spell," I said. "The Words might be rendered in magic as 'plunder.'"

"That's debatable," said M. Brayve, digging his toes into the cubicle's floral-patterned carpet. "Anyway, it's below floor 134 - floor 134's trouble now."

"Perli's a tillager of your floor."

"We did put her on the rescue queue," Perpull chimed in. He raised a clipboard. "See?"

I took the clipboard. "Eleventh. You dogs have let Ostrabawgewlus-"

"SHH," both hissed together, a matched set.

I smiled and spoke softly, which is difficult to do with a jaw clenched tight. "What are the numbers beside the names on this paper, this 'forty' beside my Perli's name?"

"Quarters until attempted rescue," said M. Brayve.

"You threaten my good humour."

"It's an estimated maximum," Perpull put in, his eyes strapped to the item on my back. "It could be sooner."

I frowned, twisting the bottom button of my coat between two fingers. "If you pups and mongrels haven't enough vertebrae between you to protect your own-"

"Those are Human Relations words."

"-I'll go down to Ostrabawgewlus's floor," both winced, "and find my Perli myself."

Mulcher Brayve raised the placating palm they must teach at the academy. "Careful - Gneiss, was it? Gneiss of where? No matter. You're no employee of a floor below 134, because no demon on floor 134 or above is an employee of those low halls."

"Two of Perli's friends already tried, Demon Rong," said Perpull. "And they came back all screwy."

"Who?"

"Fellow tillagers Daz'lyng and Owlisch. Largely built demons like yourself, too. But Red Apes, or some other terrible Creatures Electric, took the one's sight, the other's hearing."

I smiled with less force. "Stay and worry, then." I spun and walked back toward the crystal window, and this time stared firmly on the letters.

Neither dog attempted to hinder me further. Behind me, Perpull whispered to his comrade, "He's takin' the long lunch."

**Personal:**

As I stared at the window, a figure faded into the glass over the course of several seconds, translucent against the pane, the crayon words running over his navel. Long shiny black hair. A greatcoat like my own, but vibrant yellow, and with thirteen buttons where mine has three. I took a quick step back - Ostrabawgewlus, come to admire his penmanship? - but stopped when I saw the confusion animate upon the figure's face.

"What? Must that harlot, Fate, darken my schedule always at times most inopportune? This demon, which I see only wraithwise through whatever medium this be, has already one curse upon him. Well. I am presently under distress, you violent-looking fellow, and must give you your vision in short words."

"That's fine," I said.

"Interrupt me not. If thou wouldst retrieve this Perli, she-demon thou hast known since that night of full moon under sunset bush, then use thy Coat of Seven Senses in thy coming travail. Six senses must thou conceal from Ostrabawgewlus. So sayeth Withazz."

With that the figure vanished from the window.
