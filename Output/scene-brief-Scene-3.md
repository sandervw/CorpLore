# Scene Scene 3 Brief

## Premise

Igon, a demon pilgrim-knight, has descended into a sealed sub-basement of Westlake Companeye in search of a haunting oboe hold-song he heard as a child. The sub-basement houses Guilliam, an ancient "fax-machine of intelligence" that has waited centuries for someone with Igon's exact "personal song" to walk in. Guilliam uses Bluetooth necromancy: small speakers clipped to the contractually-obligated dead receive his signal and animate the corpses, while also capturing every sound the knight makes and feeding it back to the fax machine. Every kill, every hum of optimism, only harvests more of the song Igon came to find.

## Scene Structure

- POV: Third-person limited, on Igon.
- Tense: Past.
- Target wordcount: ~700 words. **At least 50% of the wordcount (~350+ words) must be devoted to the combat action itself.**

## Scene Outline

- Open with Igon entering the next chamber: a T-section hallway opening onto four server rooms (one server per room). The scene opens slowly; Igon does not realize that *all* the dead in these rooms will animate.
- Igon, talking aloud to himself, reveals his purpose for the first time in the story: he is here hunting the "beautiful, haunting, swan-like oboe hold-music" he heard as a child. (This purpose has been unstated until now.)
- The T-section hallway contains a golden water cooler artifact. Note it in passing.
- Before one server room, Igon notices a cursed carpet on the threshold and carefully steps around it. (Plant: later in the scene he will "aggro" one corpse across it, and the corpse will be blasted by the carpet's magic.)
- At each of the four servers, Igon attempts a prayer: all his known gestures of corporate obeisance to the various server-operating-systems, trying to quiet the dead.
- Each prayer fails. The failure is not because Igon cares more about the oboe than the operating systems; it is because the servers themselves are dead. The animation is not an OS function. It is the fax-machine's Bluetooth-necromancy reaching through the speakers clipped to the corpses.
- The dead shuffle in a slow orbit toward the knight, drawn like moths toward a musical flame, then attack.
- **Extended, specific combat sequence taking at least 50% of the scene's wordcount:**
  - Excruciating detail of maneuvers: name the specific footwork, draws, cuts, pivots, parries, grapples, kicks, shoves Igon uses with the Blue Glass Guillotine.
  - Excruciating detail of the dead: their specific movements, postures, gaits, how many there are (a clear number across the four rooms), what they hold or reach with.
  - Excruciating detail of bodily effects: which bones part, which joints separate, where the blade exits, what skitters across what surface, what crumples, what keeps moving after losing what.
  - **Back-and-forth structure with a mini-arc.** The fight must have ups and downs and momentum shifts; not a one-sided slaughter. Igon should be pressed, recover, be pressed again, gain ground, lose ground, and finally win. Include at least one moment where Igon is genuinely in danger or temporarily losing.
  - Include the planted beat: Igon maneuvers (or lures) a corpse across the cursed carpet, which blasts it with magic.
- After the last corpse drops, Igon finds that the main door to the next passage is sealed by a powerful Keyboard Shortcut magic-word phrase, "Have a Time." He cannot open it.
- A nearby wall in one of the four chambers has crumbled. The outer surface is stucco, but behind the stucco is rebar and concrete. Igon just barely squeezes through the broken opening, and the scene ends as he passes into whatever lies beyond.

## Already Rendered (DO NOT RE-DESCRIBE)

- Igon's full appearance, armor, helm, plume, hip-light, surcoat with painted oboe.
- The basic look of the sub-basement: stone tile floor, ancient stucco walls, plexiglass sliding doors, electric lamp light.
- The mechanic of a Bluetooth speaker clipped to a corpse's sternum by a brass clasp (Igon saw one in Scene 2). Igon does NOT yet understand the speakers animate the dead; that realization belongs to Scene 4.
- Treat tables and Worm Lockers as a concept.

## Carry-Forward State

- Igon enters from the L-shaped office of Scene 2, Guillotine shouldered, hip-light on, having just destroyed one skeleton.
- He is unhurt, energized, still in relentless optimism mode, still humming.
- He has heard the fax-machine's flat tinny voice once (through the Scene 2 speaker), and heard one sustained synthetic note that vaguely stirred his memory. He does not yet consciously connect that note to his oboe song.
- He has not yet voiced aloud *why* he is down here.

## Downstream Setups

- Every sound Igon makes (humming, talking to himself, prayers, shouts in combat) is being captured by the Bluetooth speakers on the corpses and harvested by Guilliam. Do not state this in narration; the scene should let it happen.
- Igon's first spoken declaration of his purpose (the oboe hold-song) is itself a major harvest. Render it as a hopeful, earnest moment.
- The "Have a Time" sealed door and the crumbled-wall workaround set up Scene 4's descending passage.

## Voice / Tone

- Igon's speech is warm, jovial, projecting through the helm, prone to "Ohh," and "Ahh," and courteous address even to enemies. He talks to himself often.
- Guilliam does NOT speak in this scene. The speakers may emit notes or fragments, but no dialogue from the fax-machine here.
- Tone: mythopoeic, dark, treating mundane corporate objects (servers, carpet, water cooler, stucco) as sacred or ancient. Sprinkle 1-3 word mythic or violent modifiers on ordinary nouns.
