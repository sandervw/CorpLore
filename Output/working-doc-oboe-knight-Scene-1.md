# oboe-knight: Scene 1 Working Doc

## Pre-Scene Work

**Scene Type:** Establishing. New POV (Igon), new location (sealed sub-basement beneath Westlake Companeye), opening scene of the story.

### Purpose

**Audience:** Seduce the reader with a slow, optimistic descent that hums with private joy while accreting dread. Reader should feel charmed by Igon and quietly uneasy about the door that answered him. Pose the question: what waits below, and why has it waited so long?

**Story:** Plant Igon as a faith-driven pilgrim-knight. Establish the sub-basement as undefiled by Westlake's fax-machines (foreshadow without explanation). Seed the resonance mechanism that will later wake the dead. Stage Igon and his hum at the threshold of the chamber.

### Textures

1. Cold sub-basement dark broken by faint bonefire-blue glow from the fogged plexiglass
2. Pitch-black silence of the chamber beyond the opened door

### Characters

1. **Igon of Allsode:** Pilgrim-knight, alone, helmed, walking. Warm and jovial. Faith intact, optimism unbroken. Wants to follow the swan-like oboe melody he has carried since childhood deeper into the earth; immediate goal is to get through the locked door.

### Location

A long, straight, sloped descent beneath the Westlake Companeye corporation. Rounded sandstone/stucco walls; an antediluvian office passage older than any working franchise above. Air thick with flat, pressurized HVAC fumes. The corridor terminates at a thick, fogged plexiglass sliding door, sound-triggered, that opens to a darkened chamber.

### Image-Sound Counterpoint (Optional)

Yes. Igon's warm hum and jovial voice play against the cold, undefiled dark and the silent vibration of plexiglass. Sound is welcome where the image is forbidding.

## Draft

### Threshold

The passage went down, and down, and down.

Igon of Allsode descended at a pilgrim's measured pace, alone and helmed and humming. Pressurized HVAC fumes hung thickly in the sloped corridor before him, flat and ancient and faintly sweet, the exhalation of some ductwork older than any aboveground franchise. Igon drank it in through the slot of his visor.

"Ohh, what a lovely old hallway," he said into the iron of his helmet.

### Procession

He went on. The walls were rounded sandstone, stuccoed and soft-worn at shoulder height where centuries of pauldrons had passed before his own, and his gauntleted fingers trailed the curve as a child trails a banister. Down, and down, and down. The slope was patient. His knees took it without complaint.

"They say the fax-machines of Westlake Companeye have, for all these many centuries, left this place to itself," he remarked to the iron around his face. "A great courtesy. I shall be sure to return it."

He began, then, to hum.

The melody came easily; it always had. It rose out of him soft and unhurried, a long-limbed thing that wanted only to be carried, and the helm took it and warmed it and projected it forward as a closed drone, the iron rounding his throat into a horn. He hummed and walked and hummed, and the descent went straight as a plumb-line into the dark.

Ahead of him, slowly, a paleness gathered. The corridor terminated in a sliding panel of fogged plexiglass, thick and beveled at the edges, sweating cold, lit faintly from within by a bonefire-blue glow that owed nothing to any lamp Igon could name. He stopped before it. He let the hum trail off.

He pressed his helm to the plexiglass and listened.

Nothing. No music, no breath, no scuff of inhabitance; only the pressurized fumes hissing thinly at his back.

"Hmm," he said. "Hmm."

And then, for cheer, he hummed.

The plexiglass shivered. A vibration walked the surface and rang up through his cheekbone and into the bone-shell of the helm itself, and Igon jumped back with a small undignified yelp. The door slid open without sound.

"Aha! Well met, friend lock! Hah, what fortune."
