# Scene Audit — oboe-knight-s1

## Story Fit

- **Add foreshadowing of threat** — The outline calls for foreshadowing ("with good reason") when Igon mentions the centuries-long courtesy; the draft cuts the beat before the implication lands.
  > "A great courtesy. I shall make every hallowed gesture which will - I know - satisfy the old guard employed in such a basement."

- **Clarify that Igon assumes he found the lock's secret** — The outline specifies Igon should believe he accidentally discovered the mechanism that has kept the door sealed; the draft skips that inference, weakening the dramatic irony of his false confidence.
  > "Aha! Well met to you also, friend lock! What fortune."

- **Igon's purpose is correctly withheld** — The outline requires his motivation (the oboe hold-music) to remain unstated in Scene 1. The draft honors this. No change needed.

## Internal Quality

- **Fix the run-on sentence** — Missing punctuation breaks the rhythm mid-sentence.
  > "Down, and down, and down "The handbook tells..."

- **Correct the typo** — "dity" should be "ditty."
  > "hummed a fragment of his dity to clear a budding consternation"

- **Tighten the redundancy** — "humming iron became a horn of ringing brass" and "He hummed and walked and hummed" is one beat too many on the same image.
  > "The humming iron became a horn of ringing brass. He hummed and walked and hummed..."

- **Clarify cause-and-effect at the door** — It is ambiguous whether the plexiglass opens because of Igon's hum or on its own; the outline intends the hum as the trigger. The current prose implies both readings.
  > "The plexiglass shivered. A vibration walked the surface...Igon jumped back with a small yelp. The door slid silently ajar."
