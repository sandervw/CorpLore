# Scene Scene 4 Brief

## Premise

Igon, a demon pilgrim-knight, descends into a sealed corporate sub-basement to find the "swan-like oboe hold-song" he heard as a child. Unbeknownst to him, the song is being mimicked and harvested by Guilliam, an ancient "fax-machine of intelligence" that uses Bluetooth speakers clipped to corpses to animate the Contractually Obligated dead. Every note Igon hums and every sound of his combat is captured and fed to the machine. His relentless optimism is the exact "data" Guilliam has waited millennia to drain.

## Scene Structure

- POV: Third-person limited, on Igon
- Tense: Past
- Target wordcount: ~400

## Carry-Forward State

- Igon has just won a hard fight against eleven Contractually Obligated in a T-hallway with four server rooms. He is breathing heavily through his iron visor, surcoat torn.
- He exited that chamber by squeezing through a crumbled stucco-and-rebar gap in the wall of the third server room (the main door was sealed by the magic phrase "Have a Time"). This scene picks up with him in the dark passage beyond.
- The tinny synthetic oboe note from the corpse-speakers is still ringing in his ears.
- He is still humming/talking to himself for courage, but his optimism is beginning to crack.

## Already Rendered (DO NOT RE-DESCRIBE)

- Igon's armor, helm, surcoat with hand-painted oboe, Blue Glass Guillotine, hip-light. Do not re-introduce his gear.
- The Bluetooth-speaker mechanic clipped to corpses' sternums. He has seen the speakers throughout the prior scene; this scene is where he *consciously realizes* what they are doing.
- Stucco walls with rebar/concrete underneath are already established.

## Upstream Revelations

- Reader already knows the speakers animate the dead and feed sound back to the fax-machine. Igon does NOT know this yet at scene start. His realization mid-scene is the emotional pivot.
- The oboe song he carries is being mimicked by the machine; he has been hearing the tinny version through the speakers but has not yet doubted his own song.

## Downstream Setups

- Plant the seed of doubt: this is the first scene where Igon's faith wavers. He must still press on, but the optimism should feel forced by scene's end.
- The double magic-phrase seal ("Damned if you do, damned if you don't" / "Separate or altogether") must be broken by brute force with the Guillotine, not by a magic word. This shows he is now improvising past corporate protocol.

## Scene Outline

A short transition scene; Igon begins to doubt.

- Igon descends a sloping narrow passage leading down, down, down.
  - Straight halls that turn corner after corner.
  - Walls entirely undecorated.
  - Instead of long fluorescent tubes, each hall ceiling has a single bulb dangling from a chain; weak, yellow light.
- No undead in this passage. Igon is breathing heavy from the prior fights. The "false oboe hold music" is ringing in his ears.
- During this descent, Igon realizes the machine's animating music is delivered through Bluetooth speakers clipped to the dead and embedded in the fire alarms; it resonates in the Worm Lockers and reanimates the Contractually Obligated dead.
- The descent ends in another locked sliding plexiglass door, this one sealed with a double phrase of Mighty Danger Words: **"Damned if you do, damned if you don't"** and **"Separate or altogether"**
- Igon has no choice but to break the plexiglass down by force with his Guillotine.
- Describe the sticky film of sweat, his shirt clinging coldly to his skin under his armor, his eyes stinging from the tomb dust, as he steps over the broken plexiglass.
