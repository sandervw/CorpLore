# Rafe Scigley: Scene 8

The Coordinator led them down.

Not along a corridor, nor through any passage belonging to the building's known architecture, but down -- through vertical darkness. The fluorescent tubes died one by one as they passed beneath. The carpet gave way to bare concrete, then to stone. The Lorre candidate walked close behind. Close enough that Rafe could hear the small wet sounds of his breathing.

"I don't care for this," the Lorre candidate whispered.

"Nor should you," Rafe said, without turning.

The smell reached them before the chamber did: calcium and toner ink, dry as kiln-dust, a mineral exhalation that coated the back of the throat. The Coordinator did not slow. His iron keys rang against his hip with each descending step.

Then the corridor opened.

A conference chamber. A long table of black-lacquered wood stretched from the threshold to a far end lost in shadow, and around it sat the Skeleton Crew.

They were twelve, or perhaps fourteen -- the projector light did not reach the table's full length. Each occupied an ergonomic chair with the posture of long habit. They did not move. They did not look up. Before each skeleton lay a printed meeting agenda, and at the head of the room, a pull-down screen displayed a slideshow in pale, sickly light.

The Coordinator stopped at the threshold and made a note on his clipboard.

"You will be seated," he said. "The meeting is in progress."

Rafe took the nearest empty chair. The mesh back sagged beneath him; the casters were locked to the floor. The Lorre candidate sat beside him and gripped the armrests.

The projector cast its cone across the screen. A new slide resolved:

*MANDATORY NON-DISCRIMINATION COMPLIANCE TRAINING -- MODULE 7: RESPECTFUL TERMINATION PRACTICES*

A voice began to narrate. It came from nowhere identifiable -- a droning, sexless recitation that seemed to rise from the floor itself. It spoke of equitable treatment. It spoke of inclusive language in abrupt termination proceedings. It spoke with the cadence of a burial rite.

The Skeleton Crew listened. One near the middle adjusted its posture. Another turned a page of its agenda with yellowed phalanges, the tarnished ring on its fourth finger scraping the paper. Their gem-studded lanyards caught the projector's light and scattered it in cold fragments across the walls. Before each stood a placard of hammered gold and polished jet, engraved with titles in a fiscal alphabet that predated the building, perhaps the corporation itself.

They were following the agenda.

The Lorre candidate leaned close. "They are dead," he hissed. "They are dead, and they are taking *notes*."

"They are in a meeting," Rafe corrected. He had seen it at once -- the pattern was unmistakable to a demon of his credentials. Parliamentary procedure, rigid and unbroken as any contract. The agenda dictated the flow, and the Crew was bound to it. They could no more deviate than stone could choose its own weight.

*Which meant the agenda could be leveraged.*

Rafe stood.

The Crew's empty sockets turned toward him. The narration continued.

"Point of order," Rafe said. His voice was steady, professionally pitched. "I am an external auditor, retained on contract for scope review. I move to table agenda item nine -- the audit of present-cycle personnel -- pending a quorum confirmation of the audit's jurisdictional scope."

Silence. The projector hummed.

The Lorre candidate stood too. "We should leave," he said. He reached for Rafe's sleeve. Rafe pulled his arm free.

A skeleton at the far end of the table raised one bony hand.

"The motion has been received," the Coordinator said, from his position by the door. He did not look up from his clipboard. "Parliamentary procedure requires a vote."

The Crew deliberated. No discussion -- only phalanges tapping the lacquered wood in sequence, a dry percussion that traveled the table's length like a roll call. One tap for aye. Two for nay.

Rafe did not wait for the result. He rose from his locked chair, stepped behind the row of skeletons, and moved toward the far wall, where a door was barely visible in the projector's peripheral glow.

The door was locked.

He tried the handle. Then again, full weight against the frame. The metal was cold and the lock had no keyhole, no card reader, no mechanism he could identify. Sealed by something older than hardware.

*A lesser auditor would have been discouraged. Rafe Scigley was not a lesser auditor.*

Behind him, the tapping ceased. The vote had concluded. The Skeleton Crew sat in renewed stillness along the table's length.

The Lorre candidate had not moved from his chair. The compliance slides cycled past him. His eyes were fixed on the screen, reflecting the projector's glow. He was no longer useful.

The Coordinator's pen scratched against his clipboard. The next slide advanced.
