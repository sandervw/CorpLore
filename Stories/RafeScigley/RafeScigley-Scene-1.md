# Rafe Scigley: Scene 1 - "The Cubicle Garden"

Mr. Rafe Scigley moved through the Cubicle Garden with the unhurried stride of a demon who belonged there. The Department of Gender-Appropriate Commination occupied a full half-floor of the eastern wing, its territory marked by a hand-lettered placard reading TEAMS 7.A THROUGH 8.G and by the particular quality of its air: recycled beyond all memory of freshness, carrying notes of stale toner and carpet adhesive and, from the floor vents, something faintly sulfurous that Rafe chose not to investigate. The carpet underfoot had been worn to a greasy sheen along the traffic paths between cubicle rows. Decades of trudging feet had ground its original pattern into indeterminacy.

Rafe approved of the place. It had the unmistakable atmosphere of a department where no one looked up from their work, where initiative had been punished so thoroughly that it no longer occurred as a possibility. The cubicle partitions rose to roughly chest height, upholstered in some institutional fabric the color of cold porridge, and behind them sat rows of demons engaged in the steady labor of commination. Whatsoever that entailed, Rafe had not troubled himself to learn.

Rafe was in the market for a Forever World. He was 'sufficient consequence', said Rafe to himself, and need not sink to level of constructing his own Forever World. He had never started one. He did not, in general, dwell on it. Yet he of course needed a world, and the fastest means of acquisition was through a donor.

He paused at the end of a row and surveyed the field. Fourth alcove from the left: a solitary demon, hunched and round-shouldered, pecking at his glyphboard with the resigned rhythm of long habit. No neighbors within earshot. Rafe straightened his sport coat, adjusted his expression to one of brisk institutional concern, and advanced.

"Good afternoon," said Rafe, arriving at the alcove's entrance with the sudden authority of a man holding a clipboard he did not, in fact, possess. "MY name is Rafe Scigley, Compliance Liaison for the Ergonomic Standards Division. I am here to conduct a spot-check."

The demon looked up. He had the flat, unblinking eyes of someone who had long ago ceased to distinguish one interruption from another. Behind him, a motivational poster hung in a cheap frame, its glass cracked diagonally: a sunrise over a parking garage, captioned SYNERGY IS A JOURNEY. Rafe noted it with private approval.

"I don't recall a spot-check this lunar cycle," the demon said.

"That is because it was unscheduled." Rafe let his gaze travel over the alcove with performative concern: the curling memos pinned to the cubicle wall, yellowed past legibility; the CRT monitor glowing its sickly phosphor green; the demon's posture, which was, admittedly, terrible. "Your lumbar angle is outside parameters. Significantly outside, if I am being candid." He reached into his jacket and produced a bonsai root hook, a curved implement with a sharpened steel tip whose original purpose had been agricultural. He held it up as one might brandish a medical instrument. The sharpened steel caught the overhead fluorescent light - one tube of which was flickering arrhythmically, casting stuttering shadows across the partition fabric. "You see this tuning gauge? Don't touch it."

The demon leaned back slightly. Somewhere two rows over, a telephone began a persistent bleating. It would ring, Rafe suspected, for the remainder of his natural life.

"What you must now do," Rafe continued, tucking the hook away and adopting a soothing cadence, "is report to the Calibration Office on the fourth mezzanine. Room 414-C. They'll run a corrective divination - I estimate it will take fifteen minutes, perhaps twenty. Nothing invasive."

"I've never heard of corrective diviniation."

"It was established last quarter, a part of the new wellness initiative." Rafe's sport coat shifted as he gestured, its pockets clinking faintly. "You shall wish to bring your intake form? They're firm about documentation."

The demon hesitated. Around them, the cubicle garden produced its ambient music: the rain-patter of keystrokes from neighboring alcoves, rising and falling in waves, punctuated by an occasional muttered oath.

"I don't have an intake form," the demon said. He turned back to the electric screen, and raised his hands to the array of glyhps before.

"Then you'll need to accquire one through a processing rite," Rafe added quickly. "That would take place upon the third mezzanine - not the fourth - in the first, rightmost door, second corridor past the water station." Rafe allowed a note of sympathy to enter his voice. "I understand the inconvenience. Believe me, I raised my own uproar when my turn came. But the mandate comes from above, and between us, Ergonomic Compliance imprints itself into a quarterly review when flouted."

The demon stood. He collected a binder from his desk with slow, heavy movements, and departed. Rafe watched him go, tracking the round-shouldered figure as it shuffled down the cubicle row and turned the corner toward the stairwell.

He was alone. The telephone two rows over continued to ring. Rafe stepped into the alcove.

The terminal screen still glowed; the employee had not touched the board of glyphs before he left. Around the terminal - just barely visible, like heat haze shimmering above summer asphalt - the Ward of User Authentication undulated. Rafe's own fingers moved to the glyphboard.

Just as rafe was about to press the longest, bottommost key of the board, the Ward flickered. It guttered, dimmed, and dissolved into nothing - the session had simply timed out. The screen reset to a generic login prompt, the phosphor green characters rearranging themselves into the departmental seal and a blinking cursor. The Forever World, whatever this employee's world had looked like, was sealed behind credentials.

Rafe heaved a melancholy a sigh. He looked sulkily at the departmental seal.

The login prompt blinked. Beneath it, filling the lower third of the display, an advertisement glared in block-letter font within a lurid red border:

**NOW HIRING - DEPARTMENT OF GENDER-APPROPRIATE COMMINATION**
**Teams 7.A through 8.G - All Positions - Apply Within**

Rafe read it twice. A slow, considering expression settled over his angular features. 

"Why not apply?" thought Rafe. The meagerest display of his professional qualities must secure for Rafe a following interview. And if he then chose to fail that interview - spectacularly enough to be memorable, modestly enough to incure no excessive demonaic wrath - he could secure for himself a speedy escort to [TODO-OFFICE-NAME]'s nearest parking garage. And from there, release. Escape.

Rafe turned from the terminal and found, beside the glyphboard, a laminated ID card on a coffee-stained lanyard, still warm from its owner's neck. The photograph had faded to a pinkish blur. The name read GORBEN FELCH, COMMINATION ASSOCIATE, TEAM 7.D. Rafe took it.
