# Rafe Scigley: Scene 10

## Full Scene

The corridor deposited him into a room he had not chosen to enter. Rafe steadied himself against the doorframe. His limbs were present. That would do.

The desk before him was enormous, built from filing cabinets crushed together under tremendous pressure until they had fused into a single geological stratum of pressed steel. Behind it, motivational posters hung in frames whose glass had fogged from the inside. The office was deep in the administrative viscera of HR, a windowless chamber carpeted in something so ancient and compressed it had become geological.

Behind the desk sat the Aychar.

It did not look up. Its swollen bulk shifted in a chair that had long since become structural. A brass nameplate on the desk read PROCESSING RITE in engraved serif, and beneath it, in smaller type, a date that predated the building.

"Ah," the Aychar said warmly. "There you are."

Rafe brushed plaster dust from the torn shoulder of his sport coat. The lining showed through. He straightened what remained.

"A regrettable outcome," he said. "I am afraid I have failed your onboarding rite entirely. Catastrophically, one might say. Three chambers, not a single binding contract signed. I trust you shall want me removed from the premises." He straightened his cuffs. "I can see myself out."

The Aychar's lips stretched wide.

"Failed?" it said. "Mr. Scigley, you have *survived*. That is not the same thing at all."

Its throat inflated with a wet, membranous sound. It spoke again.

"The Onboarding Annex is not an examination. It is a *rite*. Survival is completion." One bloated hand pressed a key on the terminal. The screen flickered to life, cycling through benefits enrollment forms. Each field had been pre-filled with Rafe's name in a typeface he had not authorized. "You have been re-oriented. Congratulations."

The Aychar's eyes darkened momentarily and then lightened again. It turned back to the terminal and began typing, as though the matter were already closed.

"No," said Rafe.

He stepped forward and rested one hand on the desk's fused surface.

The Aychar did not stop typing. "Your processing rite is already underway." It produced a laminated packet from beneath the desk and slid it forward without looking up. The packet was warm from the printer. It smelled of toner and something older. "Re-Orientation Packet, Section 1 through 14. You will find your new role assignment on page 3, your benefits summary on page 7, and your updated seniority classification on page 11."

Rafe did not touch the packet. "Updated seniority?"

"Reset, rather. All years of prior service have been recalibrated to zero for purposes of benefits accrual, pension eligibility, and parking allocation." The Aychar smiled again. The fluorescent panel above flickered. The creature's flesh rippled beneath it. "A fresh start. We find it motivates."

*Zero seniority. Zero accumulated obligations.* Rafe could feel the arithmetic assembling itself, the beautiful symmetry of a loophole presenting its open face. *Years of compounding company debt, wiped clean. All of it, gone, by their own hand.*

"Well," he said. "One must accept the results of due process. If I might simply confirm, for my own records, that the seniority reset extends to all financial encumbrances, outstanding balances, and accrued penalties..."

"It does not."

Rafe's loafers shifted on the ancient carpet.

"Company debts carry over in full." The Aychar's tone did not change. "Per the Standard Re-Orientation Addendum, Section 4, Paragraph 12: *All prior obligations survive reclassification.* Your balance, in fact, has increased." It gestured toward the door. A collection notice hung pinned to the wall beside the frame, ink still damp:

*1x Ergonomic Task Chair, Series 7, Lumbar-Adaptive: DESTROYED*
*Replacement cost: 14,772.03 terces*
*Charged to: SCIGLEY, R.*
*Referred to: Office of Mr. P. Sinocarre, January Financials Division*

Rafe read it twice.

"Additionally," the Aychar said, producing a second slip of paper, "Mr. Sinocarre has booked an immediate appointment with you. He was most specific about the timing."

The intercom above the door crackled. A voice, bright and clerical, read from what sounded like a scheduling template: "Mr. Scigley, your appointment with Mr. Sinocarre of the January Financials Division has been confirmed for immediate availability. Mr. Sinocarre is en route. Please remain at your current location."

He stood. The bonsai root hook clicked against his hip as he shifted his weight.

"Where," he said, "is my new desk assignment?"

The Aychar did not answer immediately. It pulled the laminated packet back across the desk, squared it against the terminal, and only then consulted the screen. "Gallery 11-West. Cubicle block F, Row 9."

Rafe collected the re-orientation packet and tucked it into his messenger bag. His hand brushed the rolled motivational poster he had been carrying since the first chamber. He left it where it was.

He turned right, and kept walking.
