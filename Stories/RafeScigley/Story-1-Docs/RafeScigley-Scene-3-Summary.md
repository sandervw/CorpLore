# Rafe Scigley: Scene 3 - Post-Scene Summary

## Scene Summary

Rafe enters a vast supply storage area that has grown into a humid, lightless forest of metal shelving. He camps at a junction deep in the stacks, sleeping on flattened envelopes with a bubble-wrap pillow. He wakes to a Supply Clerk who demands a requisition form. Rafe talks his way out and asks for directions to the hiring department. The clerk tells him the hiring annex no longer exists in this wing, but points him toward "Onboarding" via a hand-drawn map of routes through the stacks, warning him about a hum. Rafe follows the red line through the thinning forest. As fluorescent lights click on in sequence, the supply-forest gives way to clean corridors and a set of double doors marked ONBOARDING. A warm, promising hum pulls him forward. He walks through, believing he is on schedule.

## Ending Text

...he could make out lettering etched into the glass: **ONBOARDING -- ORIENTATION HALL C**. A low sound reached him. Not the HVAC. Not the building's respiration. A hum. Steady and warm. It seemed to emanate from behind the doors, or from the walls themselves, and it carried with it a sensation he could not name but which felt like the first day of a new appointment: the lanyard still crisp, the badge still clean, the future still a thing that could be leveraged.

*There it is. Right on schedule.*

He pushed through the doors.

## Questions Posed

- What is the hum Rafe hears, and why did the clerk warn him about it?
- The building led Rafe directly to "Onboarding" rather than the hiring department he expected. Is he walking into a trap?

## Characters Introduced or Referenced

**Rafe Scigley:** Tall, lean, rumpled sport coat, messenger bag. Self-assured schemer who sleeps in supply closets with the air of a demon checking into a hotel. Believes he is navigating the building; the building is navigating him.

**The Supply Clerk:** Small, colorless, blanched skin, permanently fogged reading glasses, cloudy badge on ancient lanyard. Speaks in inventory codes and shelf coordinates. Has become a geographic feature of the supply-forest. Gives directions with the tone of a demon who has been asked to explain rain.

## Reflection

**What worked:** The supply-forest as environment; the clerk as laconic guide; the image-sound counterpoint; the building-as-organism motif; the hum as foreshadowing.

**What was cut in review:** Nothing cut; added bubble-wrap complication, toner dust, and the shelf-coordinate chit exchange.

**What's missing:** Could use more lore about why the supply-forest exists. The clerk could hint at deeper history.
