# Rafe Scigley: Scene 4

Rafe presented himself at the processing desk. A supply clerk's hand-drawn map and a hum he could not refuse had brought him here, but he straightened his sport coat and decided he had arrived precisely when intended.

"Good morning," he said. "I understand you are hiring."

The waiting area held three other supplicants. The first was a small demon folding and refolding a crumpled attestation with hands that never stopped moving. Across from him sprawled an enormous demon, corpulent with hideous quantities of flesh, filling his waiting-room chair completely. He laughed at nothing. The third was a she-demon of composed beauty. She held a leather portfolio against her chest and was already looking at Rafe when he turned.

None of them spoke. All of them, he suspected, were listening.

Behind the processing desk, a fluorescent panel buzzed at a frequency that made printed text swim and blur. The compliance notices tacked to the cubicle partitions were unreadable. Rafe ignored this. He had spent a night in the supply-forest and walked half the morning. The plan was elegant in its simplicity: deliver a spectacularly poor interview, be judged unemployable, and locate the nearest door to the highway. He was, he felt, overqualified for the task of failing.

He adjusted his sport coat, smiled his most affable smile, and waited.

A door behind the desk opened six inches before anything touched it. Then the Aychar drifted through.

It did not walk. It floated: a swollen mass of bureaucratic flesh suspended at desk-height, bulging lips perpetually parted, a distended throat that rippled when it spoke. Its eyes were two filled-in checkboxes, perfect black squares pressed into the doughy expanse of its face. It wore no lanyard. It needed none.

"Mr. Scigley," it said. The voice was warm and procedural. "You are late."

Rafe had not given his name. He adjusted his smile.

"A minor navigational discrepancy," he said. "I trust the interview can still proceed on schedule."

"Interview," it repeated. The word came out flattened, taxonomic.

The Aychar drifted sideways along the desk, consulting a form that had materialized in its bloated fingers. Rafe tracked it, shifting his weight from one foot to the other, keeping the exit door in his periphery.

"There is no interview, Mr. Scigley." It did not look up. "Your interview was conducted and passed on the fourteenth. You were expected on the fifteenth for your Rites of Integration. It is now..." It glanced at a wall clock whose hands had been painted on. "Considerably later than the fifteenth."

"I see," said Rafe. Behind his affable mask, rapid calculations. He had applied for this position hours before he found the supply-forest, via a terminal accessed through fraud in a cubicle garden two floors above. The fourteenth had not yet occurred when he submitted the application. The fifteenth, by any reasonable lunar reckoning, was tomorrow.

He decided not to raise the point. Temporal disputes with processing departments were, in his considerable experience, unwinnable.

"A clerical misunderstanding," he offered. "I shall return on the correct date. If you could direct me to the nearest..."

"You are already on the correct date, Mr. Scigley. The correct date is the date on which you are present. This is standard." The Aychar drifted two inches closer. Its breath smelled of laminate and adhesive. "Your onboarding cohort has been waiting. Three of them are seated behind you. The fourth is you."

Rafe did not turn around. He could feel the three supplicants behind him: the small one's breathing, the large one's weight, the she-demon's silence.

"I am afraid there has been an error," Rafe said, shifting his tone to the register he reserved for inspectors, creditors, and demons who stood between him and a door. "I applied for a position I have no intention of filling. My qualifications are fabricated. My references are fictional. My entire candidacy is, if we are being candid, a sustained act of..."

"Yes," said the Aychar. "We know." Its throat rippled. "Your requisition was flagged, cross-referenced, and approved. Fabricated qualifications are not disqualifying. Fictional references are accepted in lieu of actual ones, provided they are internally consistent, which yours were not, but the threshold is low." It paused. The hum behind the walls swelled faintly. "You are hired, Mr. Scigley. Congratulations. Please proceed to the Onboarding Annex for your Rites of Integration."

Behind the Aychar, a laminated org chart hung on the cubicle wall. So many revision stickers had been applied over so many fiscal quarters that the chart had become three-dimensional: a topographic map of departmental restructurings, whole divisions entombed beneath adhesive. Rafe stared at it. His name was already on it. Somewhere, under something.

"No," he said.

He turned for the door he had entered through. It was closed. Two demons stood in front of it: squat, grey-skinned creatures in ill-fitting blazers, their clipboards held before them like bucklers. Their lanyards bore no names. Their eyes bore no interest. They were hiring demons, and they had been waiting.

"Mr. Scigley," the Aychar said behind him. "Orientation is mandatory."

The large supplicant rose from his chair. Not to help. He stood and stretched, rolling his enormous shoulders, and the motion placed him squarely between Rafe and the corridor to the left. He smiled at Rafe with genial indifference and did not move.

Rafe hit the first hiring demon with a straight palm to the clipboard, driving the laminated edge into its chin. The demon staggered. The second swung its clipboard in a lateral arc that caught Rafe across the ear. He stumbled, caught himself on the water cooler, and kicked the second demon in the knee.

It did not fall. It adjusted its blazer, consulted its clipboard, and hit him again.

He threw a waiting-room chair. He swung his messenger bag. He produced his bonsai root hook and slashed a line across one demon's blazer, exposing grey flesh beneath. They did not bleed. They advanced.

The small supplicant had retreated to the far corner. The she-demon had not moved. She had produced a visitor badge from her portfolio and was clipping it to her lapel.

It lasted perhaps ninety seconds. The hiring demons worked with clerical efficiency: one pinned his arms, the other stamped a form, and together they bore him, struggling, past the Aychar's desk and through the far door.

The corridor beyond was long, fluorescent, and smelled of carpet adhesive. The hiring demons deposited Rafe on his feet and stepped back.

His bonsai root hook was gone. He registered its absence before anything else. One lapel of his sport coat was torn. His ear rang.

The three supplicants filed through behind him, herded by a creature Rafe had not yet seen: a gaunt, towering figure in a threadbare custodial robe that brushed the floor. It left a faint track in the carpet's industrial pile. The figure's face was a ledger of deep creases and hollows, eyes set far back in the skull. Ancient. It carried a ring of iron keys and a clipboard of black leather, and it moved slowly, with the certainty of something that had maintained this wing since before the building had a name.

"I am the Facilities Coordinator," it said. Its voice was dry and precise, like paper being folded along a rule. "You will follow me. There are three chambers. You will enter each one. This is not negotiable."

The large supplicant chuckled. The small one said nothing. The she-demon's visitor badge was already clipped and level.

Rafe felt the weight of the stolen employee badge in his inside pocket, pressing against his ribs. He had taken it from a desk in the cubicle garden above. The plan it belonged to was dead, but he kept it. He was not in the habit of discarding advantages.

He followed the Coordinator. The iron keys rang with each step, echoing off the corridor walls.
