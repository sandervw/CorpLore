# Rafe Scigley: Editing Guidelines

Apply these guidelines when revising drafted scenes for Rafe's story.

## 1. Kill narrator wit
No clever similes, no winking asides, no "which he did not" qualifiers. If the narrator is being funny, cut it. The humor should come from Rafe's actions and dialogue, not from authorial commentary.

## 2. Use Rafe's own voice for interiority
Where the draft summarizes his thoughts through narration, rewrite as direct or indirect thought in his own self-important register. He flatters himself constantly.

## 3. No contractions in Rafe's dialogue
He speaks formally, almost archaically. "I am," "you shall," "that is." This is a character signature.

## 4. Swap generic terms for setting vocabulary
Calendar to lunar cycle. Alignment to divination. Processing to processing rite. The mundane-mythic register should reach into the mechanics of speech, not just description.

## 5. Prefer bluntness over elaboration
When a short declarative sentence can replace a descriptive clause, use the short sentence. "Rafe took it" over a paragraph about palming technique.

## 6. Cut closing quips and punchlines
Scenes should end on action or image, not on a bon mot. Rafe is not performing for the reader.

## 7. Add small resistance beats from secondary characters
The mark should push back through action (turning back to work, reaching for the glyphboard) rather than just asking questions passively. This forces Rafe to adapt, which reveals character.

## 8. Donor over victim
Minimize sympathy-building description for the people Rafe exploits. They are functional obstacles, not character studies.
