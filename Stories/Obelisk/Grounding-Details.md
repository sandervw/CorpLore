# Grounding Detail Categories

Eleven types of detail that ground prose. Sensory description is the resolution of the image; the rest are provenance — evidence the image came from somewhere.

## 1. Sensory / Textural
Color, temperature, grain, sound, smell. The baseline.

## 2. Particularity
A detail so specific it can only be an instance, never a category-member. Not "a doorhandle" — "a doorhandle shaped like a weasel's head." Includes proper nouns.

## 3. Traces of Human Agency
Details implying an off-page maker, owner, or decision. A chair repaired with the wrong wood; a hand-lettered sign with one corrected letter.

## 4. Wear and Accumulated Time
History written on objects. Stair treads dished by footsteps; brass polished bare where hands grab it.

## 5. Cultural Artifacts
Invented brands, slang, jokes, superstitions, songs, etiquette.

## 6. Procedural Authority
How work actually gets done: order of operations, jargon, the tool used wrong on purpose.

## 7. Malfunction
The drawer that sticks, the gate you lift to latch. Reality is 30% broken.

## 8. The Gratuitous Detail
Barthes' "reality effect": details with no plot or thematic function.

## 9. Quantification
Odd-numbered specificity: "forty rods," "eleven minutes."

## 10. Embodiment
Characters who itch, sweat, shift weight off a bad knee.

## 11. Economic Texture
What things cost, what's scarce, what's hoarded or counterfeit.

---

**Highest-leverage for anti-repetition work:** categories 3, 4, 7, and 8. LLM prose defaults heavily to category 1 and almost never produces malfunctioning or gratuitous detail unprompted.
