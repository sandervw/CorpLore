# Koschei — Canon Summary (for LLM context)

Plain, structured summary of Koschei-Draft.md. Every line of established canon is captured here, organized so a model can recall the world, the characters, the route, and the unresolved threads without rereading the source. Bold marks the single most load-bearing fact of each section; otherwise styling is avoided.

## Core World Canon

- **Obelisk** is the setting: a vast vertical structure (a "bulb"/tower) whose interior is divided into 1,056 stacked planes called "heavens." The upper 923 are civilized heavens; the bottom 133 planes are the **hells**. In-universe, all people are called demons (male) and she-demons (female) — this is the ordinary word for a person, not a supernatural creature.
- People live and work in "cubes" (each cube is larger inside than out). "Scaffolds" are the exterior farm terraces where "tillagers" grow crops for the whole of Obelisk.
- Each heaven is a floor with its own boroughs, cultures, and geography, lit by artificial "sun tubes" and "moon tubes." It never rains in Obelisk; rust comes from condensation (the motif phrase "Condensation rust").
- **Numbering shorthand is critical and stated explicitly**: "fifty three" always means the two-hundred-and-fifty-third heaven (253), never 553 or 753; "thirty four" means 134; "hells" always means the lower 133 planes. Only the nearest floors matter in a demon's life.
- **Engineers** are the setting's magic practitioners; a "hedge engineer" is a self-taught hedge-wizard (how Gneiss describes himself).
- **Words of Mighty Danger** are powerful spells / nigromancy, written in indigo crayon. Ostrabawgewlus's "In Arrears" is one.
- **Replicas** are ancient magical artifacts/weapons manufactured by the long-defunct "Obelisk Replicas" company in an early age when heaven 134 was the foremost heaven. There are 48 "capsule vaults," each holding ~10,000 replicas: 47 are lost in the hells, vault 48 survives in heaven 134. Named examples: Pulse Beacon, Amulet of a King, Lightsaber, Protein Burner, Gatling Laser Axe, and the Coat of Seven Senses.
- **Creatures Electric** are the monsters of the hells. Named so far: Wheevils (half-demon-tall rodents, pink snout, long ears, square bone-cracking teeth, twiggy bird-legs, swarm in thousands, eat copper), Red Apes (pink-glowing ape things taller than demons; they attack anyone who speaks English), Jurasik (a hairless marsh-yak with grated-iron hide and iron hooves, a battering ram), Tuples, Mr. Montag, "a Butler" (unexplained; a cave-thunder roar, *HHHGGGGUUUUURR*), Brain Bier, Folded-Shape-Most-Miserable. Rumored also: man-faced swine, a jelly that devours boroughs, a mushroom forest that eats iron, an immortal centipede that fears fire, assassin wasps, ghosts, rattlesnakes.
- **Lifts** are timeless elevators spanning many heavens: no time passes outside while one rides, and an a cappella quartet (lead, bass, tenor, baritone) endlessly harmonizes five songs.
- **Mulchers** are each heaven's police ("dogs of law"), organized under Bosses. A "Boss" is a local ruler. Lord Mynoot of a Green Rod is Executive Master over heavens 34 through 9.
- **Descent is sealed**: magic bars every Good-realm path from dropping below heaven 134; the single lift that once went down has been broken six years.
- **Waygers** are nomad, meat-eating demons/she-demons who roam the lowest peaceful lands and know the hells.
- **Wickerdemons** are pale "wick guild" members who carry light in their palms; "Lit Going" is taxed in the lightless region.
- **Bogarts** are animate lock/eye mechanisms that guard doors and cells, opened by key-cards swiped over their "eye."

## Character Index

- **Gneiss Rong "Big"** — protagonist and first-person narrator. Large, strong demon; a "hedge engineer"; educated in occult lore and Obelisk history. Wears a grey Coat of Seven Senses (a cursed replica) and, by Ch.5, wields a Gatling Laser Axe.
- **Sirrel** — a she-demon tillager of heaven 253, famed for her beauty ("much pulchritude"); Gneiss's love interest; abducted by Ostrabawgewlus.
- **Ostrabawgewlus** — antagonist: an ancient demon spell lord, ~175+ years old, author of the treatise *Vampires of a White Pinhole Sky* (still the defining work of modern astrology). His mark "In Arrears" (indigo crayon) is his signature of plunder/abduction. His lair is in hell 130.
- **Mulcher Brayve** and **Mulcher Laym** — mulchers of heaven 253 who warn Gneiss against descending to the hells.
- **Myoot** and **Kallus** — two bull-shaped tillager demons of heaven 253 who tried to rescue Sirrel before the story opens. Myoot is blind ("moonmilk eyes"); Kallus is deaf. They lost their senses in the hells (Myoot in 133, Kallus in 132). Their Boss Hoalsail resettled them into a ruin-cube as punishment for "an aural niggle." They dictate Gneiss's map.
- **Plecent** — Myoot's wife.
- **Hoortulon** — a financial lord who grants Gneiss caravan transport; **Femik** — his huge, mentally-childlike son (the "Seeyou!" game); **Edji** — Gneiss's goat.
- **Mulchers of heaven 134** — First Mulcher Iternle Dovich (senior, three-tooth badge); Chief Mulcher Morel Ulakhan (boy-faced, rosewood badge of two lizards, one white one puce, tails tied); Mulcher Aybul (red sideburns); Mulcher Seaslesc (one-eyed she-demon, sunflower eyepatch); Mulcher Joiyuss Dustman (blonde braid; frees Gneiss from his cell).
- **The Seeming of Withazz** — a honeybee-yellow-coated wraith in the window who delivers the prophecy.

## Chapter 1 — Of A Crayon

Gneiss climbs to heaven 253 at dawn (a heaven of mushrooms, millet, tillagers, much eating) to visit Sirrel and finds her gone, with "In Arrears" written in indigo crayon on her window. The words smell of fresh-mown grass and dung — the scent of Words of Mighty Danger (nigromancy). He recognizes them as Ostrabawgewlus's scrawl. Mulchers Brayve and Laym explain that "In Arrears" recurs through history as a mark of plunder (Lord Wispurus's scaffold embargo, Nancing's Yellow Ledgers, the golden-teeth decades, wars between heavens), and that Sirrel is eleventh in line among the heaven's missing (after Sower, abducted by cousins in the Ruddylord's halls on 217; and Mamuth, who vanished from a locked cube leaving only a ring of ginger salt). They warn Gneiss never to descend, noting Myoot and Kallus tried and returned whole in body but one blind, one deaf. A wraith then appears in the window.

**The prophecy (Seeming of Withazz) — the book's quest skeleton:**

> "Thou shalt seekest thy jewel of an oyster, treasured from a night of round moon, spent behind a bush of heavy tomatoes. If thou would have this treasure back in hand, thou must pass three doors, find four friends, fight four foes, and hide from six of seven."

Canon established: the heaven-numbering shorthand; "In Arrears" as Ostrabawgewlus's plunder-signature; his identity and 175-year-old astrology treatise; Myoot and Kallus's prior failed rescue; the three-doors / four-friends / four-foes / six-of-seven prophecy.

## Chapter 2 — Of A Map

Gneiss visits Myoot and Kallus in their ruin-cube (a former borough storehouse, farthest cube in the row, never cleaned). Myoot is blind (uses a braille letterboard and a "looking lens" running a "forever world" video-game-like avatar); Kallus is deaf and has just burned both hands on a toffee furnace. By flattery and begging, Gneiss wins their help, and they dictate a map, which he draws on the blank back of a "free tax" scroll (No. 11-B).

Route / canon:
- Route: from heaven 253 (an L-shaped chief road; the Surrance Consortium robs travelers and must be avoided via a branch road through the 53-mulcher precinct), descending through ~100+ heavens to hell 130.
- A timeless Lift drops 19 heavens from the L's far tip.
- En route: the wall of Sempraland, a goat-land and steam marsh (after a ~1,200-pace marble step behind a cyclopean stone wall), Vendingrow, a sea beast of Oikodespotes Lake, non-translucent south crystal panes.
- **Law: no sleeping in any space not ringed by a pentacle of salt.**
- A "grommet" on the map marks a strange memory-machine that only says "ow" and requires a wooden cylinder to pass.
- Myoot reached hell 133 and lost his eyes to the monster guarding its gate; Kallus reached 132 (found the gate open) and lost his ears to a lower monster. Each of two ogres guards a gate of those two hells and takes a sense before opening passage; they guard the surest paths, though secret tunnels may exist.
- A hell may be flooded with too-blue water reflecting pink bodies (Red Apes).
- **Ostrabawgewlus's lair is in hell 130**, learned from the Waygers.
- Gneiss's weapon is revealed: the **Gatling Laser Axe** — a single crescent-moon head of auld metal, thick along the trunk, wrapped in black leather, toxic neon glowing through housings in head and haft, six tubes at the pommel. Note: he already carries it here, before the vault scenes of Ch.5 (see Open Threads).

## Chapter 3 — Of A Way I Did Go

Gneiss travels the map. Day one: 20 miles through the Surrance Consortium to a bedding-cube by a Lift. He rides the timeless Lift, then prevails on Hoortulon (a financial lord who does not travel himself) for caravan transport by goatback over the steam Bog of Canne (marble vents breathing warm mist). Each night he plays the "Seeyou!" game with Femik — if he flinches, Hoortulon has promised to eat him. He parts at Ives on Stall. He stops at Credleburg (heaven 212) for the Feast Day of Saint Mantyck, then leaves over a south wattle fence. He wanders heaven 160 for ~24 days in sticky summer. He descends into A Belt Debatable, pays a wickerdemon seven stamps of credit to be guided through, then reaches heaven 134 and is arrested.

Canon established:
- Lifts are timeless (no time outside; a cappella quartet loops five songs, echoing from a tin-mine mouth).
- Hoortulon, Femik, the "Seeyou!" game, the goat Edji.
- **Credleburg / Saint Mantyck**: the saint used Words of Mighty Danger to melt an enemy army (the Brotherhood of Black Swords) with intensified sun rays; fused black bones still lie east and west. Feast foods: portabellos, porcinis, chanterelles, honey, goat's milk, sorrel-and-tarragon meat pies, garlic pudding, rice. Forty demons at tables carved with forty years of feasters' names.
- **A Belt Debatable**: three heavens with no sun or moon tubes, perpetual cloudy night, evening-voice speech, light only in a wickerdemon's palm, "Lit Going" taxed.

## Chapter 4 — Of A Gaol

Gneiss is imprisoned in heaven 134 (the lowest, oldest heaven, "lived atop maybe seven histories"). The charge: coveting a Replica kept in the heaven's capsule vault, which marks him a suspected "Bad demon." In the barracks outside his cell a crisis erupts: the "rivers of copper and lightning" carrying speech between heavens have gone silent — apparently gnawed by wheevils. The mulchers arm (cleavers, stampers, gas bottles), then repel a first wave of wheevils and three Red Apes, losing four of their own. Joiyuss Dustman arrives, sole survivor of a chow-run, pursued by a second wave; a Jurasik smashes through and crushes a mulcher's head. Gneiss, still locked up, is freed by Joiyuss using key-cards on the door's bogart, and the two fight clear on a butcher's trolley, retreating with the survivors through a steel vault door (opened by two platinum cards) into the capsule vault.

Canon established:
- **Coat of Seven Senses**: cursed, grey, one of Obelisk's eldest replicas; has power, but none against a strong cell (its invisibility is used in Ch.5).
- **Communication infrastructure**: "rivers of copper and lightning" carry voice between heavens; wheevils eat copper.
- Mulcher armaments: cleavers and **stampers** (lead-rod projectiles fired by gas bottles); the alarm is a "Lamp Mouth" tocsin (red light of danger).
- The crime of coveting a vault Replica; release conditions (prove Goodness, pay a fine of "fit pennies," or a virgin butterfly makes a wish).
- **Gatling Laser Axe provenance**: auction-only, first bid ~1,200 CUs, made in one age of eld, never sold nor swung ("stillborn"); Gneiss saw it in an old Obelisk catalogue shown by a cubeless demon at a shrine of Saint Sylvur of Diamond-on-Moore.
- Heaven 134's Boss: **Lord Mynoot of a Green Rod**, Executive Master of heavens 34–9.
- New Creatures: Jurasik (steel bull), Tuples (named), plus the rumor-list of hell-beasts.
- A foul "rotting-whales" stench is noted — an unexplained omen.
- "A Butler" is first spoken by Gneiss as the *HHHGGGGUUUUURR* rumble is heard.

## Chapter 5 — Of A Gatling Laser Axe

Inside vault 48 (total darkness), the survivors light their way with scattered lamps — including Gneiss's "herald box," which belonged to his grandmother. The vault is a round brick tower with a bottomless drop, marble plinths, and descending steps lined with niches, one replica per niche (~10,000 total). The jurasik batters the door while the mulchers argue: Gneiss urges them to arm themselves with the relics; Ulakhan refuses ("Retired is retired," law and precedent); Dovich objects that untried, maybe-cursed relics in untrained hands are as dangerous as giving a child a starfire-bubble wand. The door bursts and wheevils pour in. Gneiss buttons his Coat of Seven Senses (bottom rough blind-eye-colored, middle grey, top black) and vanishes from sight, hearing, smell, and touch; he descends to the 13th niche, retrieves the Gatling Laser Axe (~10 pounds), and annihilates the Creatures with strobing green beams, melting the jurasik's steel. He reveals himself, refuses to surrender the axe ("Capiche?"), and a heavyset she-demon mulcher seizes a second relic — the **Protein Burner** — to weld the vault doors shut. The cave-thunder *HHHGGGGUUUUURR* sounds again; Gneiss calls it "A Butler," but no one asks what he means.

Canon established:
- Vault 48 is the oldest of demonism's heritage; the other 47 vaults (~10,000 replicas each) are lost in the hells.
- **Coat of Seven Senses buttoning sequence** (bottom→middle→top) grants total sensory invisibility (sight, hearing, smell, touch); a damp, blood-jumping sensation while active.
- **Protein Burner** — a replica from the fable *MORTIS*: a half-demon, half-bird star-being sacrificed itself and was cut into eight pieces, which eight engineers forged into Mighty tools to kill a deathless giant; it fires an ivory flame hot enough to weld steel.
- **"A Butler"** — a looming, still-unexplained threat producing the cave-thunder roar; Gneiss alone recognizes it.
