# Koschei

## Scene 1 - The Empty Office

**Arrival:**

I reached two hundred and fifty third heaven, a floor of mushrooms, of millet, of tillagers, of much eating, and of little bother, very early. A shine of dawn hugged Obelisk on its tall east face. I came to west-facing Warm's Route, however, and that looks west through windows of a crystal, of much thickness, clarity, and maybe one hundred big man's feet tall they are: that vast height means nothing in Obelisk's umbra of western dawn.

I came of a mind to lay eyes on Perli, she-demon I well know,

*Twenty-eight tillager families in brick cubicles, a voluntary tax of millet and mushrooms rendered to the floor's Mulcher unit. Worn carpet runs between a timeless elevator and a marble coffee altar with its chalice cult. Gneiss mocks the ceremony aloud, then sights Perli's one-bed cubicle and her empty violet velvet chair.*

**Discovery:**

*In Arrears.*

I withheld my sight from the two words. They were written in indigo crayon on the crystal window directly opposite Perli's cubicle, fine and neat in a way no child's wall graffiti ever managed. I had needed only the glimpse. If the smell still hanging in the air - fresh-mown field grass over something like skunk spray, or dung - had not announced Danger in the words, the handwriting would have sufficed. I knew it from a 175-year-old treatise of astrology, *Vampires of the White Pinhole Sky*, and I knew the storied demon it belonged to.

"Ostrabawgewlus," I said, and laved the breadth of my chin. "It seems you've stolen my Perli. If she is jailed now in your black and stinking domain, I'll-"

**Parley:**

"Stop," a voice barked. "Those Mighty Words hold a magic obscure, and I've no wish to be eavesdropped by the demon you named."

I turned my sight back to Perli's cubicle, which had grown two mulcher deputies while I was busy addressing the window. The barker, M. Brayve, held a hand over his eyes and spoke to his fellow out of the corner of his mouth. "How do you suppose it's forged?"

"Forged?" replied Mulcher Perpull - a head shorter than Brayve, two shorter than yours truly, Big Gneiss Rong, and forty thin pounds lighter. "What's forged?"

"The indigo crayon. It's a powerful wax."

Perpull, not so versed in the occult as his better, or as me, turned and stared directly at the fine words, almost invisible against the shadowed blue dawn of Obelisk's western face. He scratched a freckle. "Maybe- Suppose he found some kind of 'waxworks' of replicas. There'd be plenty of colors to pick from."

"Hmm. What about the crayon's shape."

**Argument:**

I marched through the cubicle opening and stood before the two of them. "What matters is the spell," I said. "The Words might be rendered in magic as 'plunder.'"

"That's debatable," said M. Brayve, digging his toes into the cubicle's floral-patterned carpet. "Anyway, it's below floor 134 - floor 134's trouble now."

"Perli's a tillager of your floor."

"We did put her on the rescue queue," Perpull chimed in. He raised a clipboard. "See?"

I took the clipboard. "Eleventh. You dogs have let Ostrabawgewlus-"

"SHH," both hissed together, a matched set.

I smiled and spoke softly, which is difficult to do with a jaw clenched tight. "What are the numbers beside the names on this paper, this 'forty' beside my Perli's name?"

"Quarters until attempted rescue," said M. Brayve.

"You threaten my good humour."

"It's an estimated maximum," Perpull put in, his eyes strapped to the item on my back. "It could be sooner."

I frowned, twisting the bottom button of my coat between two fingers. "If you pups and mongrels haven't enough vertebrae between you to protect your own-"

"Those are Human Relations words."

"-I'll go down to Ostrabawgewlus's floor," both winced, "and find my Perli myself."

Mulcher Brayve raised the placating palm they must teach at the academy. "Careful - Gneiss, was it? Gneiss of where? No matter. You're no employee of a floor below 134, because no demon on floor 134 or above is an employee of those low halls."

"Two of Perli's friends already tried, Demon Rong," said Perpull. "And they came back all screwy."

"Who?"

"Fellow tillagers Daz'lyng and Owlisch. Largely built demons like yourself, too. But Red Apes, or some other terrible Creatures Electric, took the one's sight, the other's hearing."

I smiled with less force. "Stay and worry, then." I spun and walked back toward the crystal window, and this time stared firmly on the letters.

Neither dog attempted to hinder me further. Behind me, Perpull whispered to his comrade, "He's takin' the long lunch."

**Personal:**

As I stared at the window, a figure faded into the glass over the course of several seconds, translucent against the pane, the crayon words running over his navel. Long shiny black hair. A greatcoat like my own, but vibrant yellow, and with thirteen buttons where mine has three. I took a quick step back - Ostrabawgewlus, come to admire his penmanship? - but stopped when I saw the confusion animate upon the figure's face.

"What? Must that harlot, Fate, darken my schedule always at times most inopportune? This demon, which I see only wraithwise through whatever medium this be, has already one curse upon him. Well. I am presently under distress, you violent-looking fellow, and must give you your vision in short words."

"That's fine," I said.

"Interrupt me not. If thou wouldst retrieve this Perli, she-demon thou hast known since that night of full moon under sunset bush, then use thy Coat of Seven Senses in thy coming travail. Six senses must thou conceal from Ostrabawgewlus. So sayeth Withazz."

With that the figure vanished from the window.

## Scene 2 - The Blind and the Deaf

**Arrival:**

I sniffed out that pair tillagers mentioned by the dogs. I introduced myself to Daz'lyng's wife, Eelastyk, at the entry of their cubicle. I said, "I'm a special friend of Perli's," and you bet your bells that was a tactless thing to say, to the wife of a demo who went willingly to rescue another woman from the lost floors. Daz'lyng's wife told me that her husband was busy, and rolled their cubicle door shut. I therefore was made to enter the cubicle secretly, by scaling the back brick panel, and slipping unseen and unheard into the plastic yard. (Unseen, and unheard by any, not merely the blind and the deaf.)

I discovered both Daz'lyng and the other tillager, Owlisch, within the cube's commodious prime workchamber, in a corner of the lot facing the wide crystal windows. The pair were engaged at a terminal screen, with the blind one, Daz'lyng, hunting and pecking over the worn studs of a beaten braille keyoard, while Owlish said, loud enough for the whole department to hear it seemed, "No, no, move your finger up a bit. You're pressing 'S', not 'W'. Your stuck in a coal cave again you idiot, find the spacebar and jump out." On the screen I could see the 3D rendering of Daz'lyng's forever world.

**Parley:**

Like the irascible goat I made myself immediately known to the pair, you will perceive my meaning, and then I said, "Big Gneiss Rong, gentlemen, is the name my fellow demons gave me. That's a nice world you have on your screen, but I'll ask you to return to the present one for a moment."

"What?" said Owlisch.

"We're overcapacitated," said Daz'lyng.

Shortly, with the assistance of a pencil and mundane words, I made clear to both my relationship with Perli and my mission. "And the dogs," I went on, "you know, the mulchers I mean, are much too domesticated to for anything more than knocking a few grandmother on the head."

"You'll never get past the red apes," said Daz'lyng.

"The five red apes guarding the way down," said Owlisch, completing his fellow tillager's thought on the instant, though I had yet to write anything down.

"Red apes are mindless, glowing beasts," I said. "I know no story of a red ape taking a demon's eyes or nose."

"Best to stay on the upper floors then, if you don't know your about a few creature," said Daz'lyng. "And I told you, Owlisch here is telling me my keyboard lessons."

"We're busy learning keyboard lessons right now," echoed Owlisch, louder.

"Something about my Perli convinced you pair to travel over a hundred floors, more than ten-thousand feet, down from your home office."

"One or two demons do the work of a crowd," said Daz'lyng.

"My Perli is a volunteering soul. She has said that she sometimes pays this 'voluntary tax' of your department, more than her share, and goes hungry some nights."

Daz'lyng shifted in his chair. "She's paid for everyone here at least once, you know, and no one asked her to give up her tomatoes and mushrooms."

**Mapmaking:**

"If we draw you a map, Big Rong," shouted Owlisch, "will you leave us to build our world?"



Dialogue-only who's-on-first: Daz'lyng dictates landmarks he cannot draw, Owlisch draws turns he cannot hear, Gneiss the sweating relay. Throwaway landmarks spent freely - Stairwell Grommet, the Xerox Chapel, Vending Row. The map converges on floor 130, three sealed doors between. Cultural: map drawn on the reverse of a Voluntary Taxation receipt, Form 11-B. Particularity: Daz'lyng's landmark, "the copier that says OW when it staples."




*He asks the way down. Both refuse, reciting the Red Apes story too loudly, near-rehearsed (subtext - the true price shames them). What turns them: Perli's past kindness, one concrete remembered favor apiece. Economic: Perli covered Owlisch's millet tax three harvests running, unasked. Quantification: both tellings number exactly five Red Apes, never varying, suspiciously identical.*
