# Koschei

## Scene 1 - The Empty Office

**Arrival:**

On the westernmost side of floor 253, running along Cookie Boulevard, along the hundred-foot-high, thousand-foot wide windows of dense crystal; windows which stare out and down over layer upon layer of the wooden platforms lining the flanks of Mighty Obelisk; windows which, in this particular office, cast rainbows upon the sere and worn carpet running between a timeless elevator and an altar of marble (whereat a silver chalice full of last-century's coffee has established a minor cult); on this floor and under the gleam of these crystal windows, lies a local department of tillage. And the tillagers of this department - twenty-eight in number they are, mostly families, each with their own two-or-three bed cubicle of rustic bricks, running along Cookie Boulevard, facing toward the broad windows - stand out from Obelisk's other departments of tillage by way of their 'voluntary taxation'. Each family, upon their return from the morning harvest at the terrace gardens, collects into a large bowl of blue plastic a portion of the day's millet and mushrooms. These they gather and deliver to floor 253's Mulcher unit.

Big Gneiss Rong had just passed the front opening of the third brick cubicle as the villagers were gathering inside, preparing the morning tax. Saying to himself, in no indoor voice, "Of all the sheepiest things I've seen of the demon workers of Obelisk, this dinky ceremony must-" Gneiss halted his speech. His pewter eyes had just scanned ahead, to the fourth cubicle, the only one-bed on Cookie Boulevard. Over the brick half-wall, expecting to see Pearli, Geniss's eyes had instead sighted an office chair, upholstered in violet velvet, empty.

---

**Discovery:**

*In Arrears.*

Gneiss's withheld his sight from two the words, written in indigo crayon, like some child's wall graffiti, on the portion of crystal window directly opposite of Perli's cubicle. If the smell still hanging in the air - like fresh mown field grass, but with something underneath like skunk spray or dung - had not hinted at Danger in the words, the handwriting would have sufficed. Gneiss, in the brief glimpse before he had turned his eyes, had recognized the fine, neat writing, and the storied demon to which it belonged, from a 175-year-old treatise of astrology: *Vampires of the White Pinhole Sky*.

"Ostrabawgewlus," Gneiss said. He laved the breadth of his chin. "It seems you've stolen my Perli. If she is jailed now, in your black and stinking domain, I'll-"

**Parley:**

"Stop," a voice barked. "Those Mighty Words hold a magic obscure, and I've no wish to be eavesdropped by the demon you named."

Gneiss turned his sight back to Perli's cubicle. Two mulcher deputies stood within the space. The one who had just spoken, M. Brayve, held his hand over his eyes, and spoke to the other out of the corner of his mouth. "How do you suppose it's forged?"

"Forged?" replied Mulcher Perpull. He stood a head shorter than M. Brayve, two shorter and forty thin pounds lighter than Gneiss. "What's forged?"

"The indigo crayon. It's a powerful wax."

M. Perpull, evidently not a versed in the occult as Gneiss or M. Brayve, turned and stared directly at the fine words, almost invisible against the shadowed blue dawn of Obelisk's western face. He scratched a freckle. "Maybe- Suppose he found some kind of 'waxworks' of replicas. There'd be plenty of colors to pick from."

"Hmm. What about the crayon's shape."

**Argument:**

Gneiss marched through the cubicle opening to stand before the two. "What matters is the spell," he said. "The Words might be rendered in magic as 'plunder'."

"That's debatable," said M. Brayve. He dug his toes into the cubicles floral-patterned carpet. "Anyway, it's below floor 134 - floor 134s trouble now."

"Perli's a tillager of your floor."

"We did put her on the rescue queue," M. Perpull chimed in. He raised a clipboard. "See?"

Gneiss took the clipboard. "Eleventh. You dogs have let Ostrabawgewlus-"

"SHH," both mulchers hissed together.

Gneiss smiled and spoke softly, which was difficult to do with a haw tightly clenched. "What are the numbers beside the names on this paper, this 'forty' beside my Perli's name?"

"Quarters until attempted rescue," said M. Brayve.

"You threaten my good humour."

"It's an estimated maximum," M. Perpull put in, his eyes fixed upon the item strapped to Big Gneiss Rong's back. "It could be sooner."

Gneiss frowned, twisting with two fingers hte bottom button of his coat. "If you pups and mongrels haven't enough vertebrae between you to protect your own-"

"Those are Human Relations words."

"-I'll go down to Ostrabawgewlus's floor," the mulchers winced, "and find my Perli myself."

Mulcher Brayve raised a palm in a placating gesture. "Careful, Gneiss, was it? Gneiss of where? No matter, you're no employee of a floor below 134, because no demon on floor 134 or above is an employee of those low halls."

"Two of Perli's friends already tried, Demon Rong," said M. Perpull. "And they came back all screwy."

"Who?"

"Fellow tillagers Daz'lyng and Owlisch. Largely built demons like yourself too. But Red Apes, or some other terrible Creatures Electric, took one's sight, another's hearing."

Gneiss smiled with less force. "Stay and worry then." He spun and walked back towards the crystal window, now staring firmly on the letters.

Neither mulcher attempted to hinder Gneiss further. "He's takin' the long lunch," M. Perpull whispered to his comrade.

**Personal:**

As Gneiss stared at window, a figure faded into the glass over the course of several seconds. The demon appeared translucent against the pane, with the crayon words running over his navel. Had long shiny black hair. He wore a greatcoat, similar to Gneiss's, but vibrant yellow in color, and with thirteen button instead of three. Gneiss took a quick step back, initially alarmed - might this be Ostrabawgewlus? - but stopped when he saw the apparent look of confusing upon the animate face of the figure.

"What? Must that harlot, Fate, darken my schedule always at times most inopportune? This demon, which I see only wraithwise through whatever medium this be, has already one curse upon him. Well, I am presently under distress, you violent looking fellow, and must give you your vision in short words."

"That's fine," said Gneiss.

"Interrupt me not. If thou woulds't retrieve this Perli, she-demon thou hast known since that night of full moon under sunset bush, then use thy Coat of Seven Senses in thy coming travail. Six senses must you conceal from in Ostrabawgewlus. So sayeth Withazz."

With that the figure upon the window vanished.
