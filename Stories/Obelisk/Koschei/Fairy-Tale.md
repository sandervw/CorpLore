# Koschei the Deathless - Fairy Tale

Tzar Bel-Belianin's wife, the Tzaritza, vanishes — stolen by the wizard Kastchey, who can't be killed by ordinary means because he keeps his life hidden outside his own body. The Tzar sends his three sons out one by one to find her.
The two eldest, Alexé and Dimitry, ride off with gold and armies but lose everything along the way. At three river crossings guarded by monstrous ferrymen, each pays the toll demanded — a hand, then a foot — before finally losing their nerve at the third river, where the price is a head. Both slink home maimed and empty-handed.
The youngest son, Petr, asks for nothing but a sword and a horse of his own choosing (picked by testing the royal herd in the sea). Guided by an old man and woman at a forest's edge, he reaches the same three rivers — but instead of paying, he kills each ferryman outright. Using iron claws, he scales a mountain so tall it takes a month to climb, reaching Kastchey's four palaces (copper, silver, gold, pearl), each holding a captive maiden who begs to be freed later. In the pearl palace he finds his mother, hides beneath her cloak, and over three visits she tricks Kastchey into revealing his true secret: his life is sealed in an egg, inside a duck, inside a hare, inside a log floating on a distant island.
Petr sails there, and with help from animals he'd earlier spared — a bear, an otter, a hawk, and a rescued fish — retrieves the egg. He crushes it, killing Kastchey instantly. He frees his mother and all three maidens, returns home a hero, marries the golden-palace maiden, and eventually inherits the throne — while his cowardly brothers marry the other two.
