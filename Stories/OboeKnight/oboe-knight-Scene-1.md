# oboe-knight: Scene 1

The passage went down, and down, and down.

Igon of Allsode descended at a pilgrim's measured pace, alone and helmed and humming. Pressurized HVAC fumes hung thickly in the sloped corridor before him, flat and ancient and faintly sweet, the exhalation of some ductwork older than any aboveground franchise. Igon drank it in through the slot of his visor.

"Ohh, what a lovely old hallway," he said into the iron of his helmet.

The walls were rounded sandstone, stuccoed: and Igon's gauntleted fingers trailed the gritty, worn surface as a child trails a banister. Down, and down, and down "The handbook tells that Westlake Companeye have, for all these many centuries, left this place to itself," he remarked to the empty space. "A great courtesy. I shall make every hallowed gesture which will - I know - satisfy the old guard employed in such a basement."

Igon began to hum. The melody came easily; it always had. It rose out of him, a long-limbed melody, and his iron full helmet took the tune and warmed it and projected it forward as a closed drone, and the humming iron became a horn of ringing brass.

Ahead of him, slowly, a paleness gathered. The corridor terminated in a sliding panel of fogged plexiglass, thick and beveled at the edges, sweating cold, lit faintly from within by a bonefire-blue glow. Igon stopped before it. He let his melody peter. He pressed the side of his helm to the plexiglass.

Nothing. No music, no breath, no scuff of inhabitance; only the pressurized thrumming of the HVAC.

"Hmm," said Igon. "Hmm." And then hummed a fragment of his ditty to clear a budding consternation.

The plexiglass shivered. A vibration walked the surface, passed through his helmet, and tickled his cheekbone. Igon jumped back with a small yelp. The door slid silently ajar.

"Aha, well met to you also, friend lock! So you have revealed your little secret to me then?."

Behind him the plexiglass slid shut. No clatter, no catch; only a soft seating of the panel into its frame. Igon turned and regarded the sealed door. He set his helm an inch from the col, cold plexiglass, and hummed another measured phrase. The plexiglass shivered obligingly, and the door slid ajar a second time.

"How splendid. We are well-acquainted now."

Igon let the door shut again. Beyond him now lay the chamber. From the dimly-lit, blue space, there came no answer to his tresspass.
