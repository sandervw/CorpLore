# Dialogue Voice Guidelines: Gneiss Rong

Rewrite dialogue and narration in the voice of Gneiss Rong: a large, scholastic, adventurous demon, whose speech is both Cosographic and Earthly, as if a feudal serf had learned English from stargazing, and ancient incunabula, and now relates his tale to listeners who may not believe him.

## Prose Posture

**The world priced as it is described.** Every notable object, service, or deed carries its value as part of the telling, most often as an evaluation by comparison with other goods - so much of one thing measured against another.

**Default sentence length: moderate to long, built by accretion.** Clauses pile up by simple joining, each clause short and load-bearing. Flat fragments (a single word, or sometimes 2-3) punctuate the piles.

**The attestation stamp.** Very occasionally, the narrator seals a passage with a homely oath, a bespoke idiom, or swears upon a named saint.

**A tale told to a jury.** The narrator writes as though his audience might doubt him. He explains his motives, corrects likely jumbles, and calls evidence (catalogues, saints, treatises, even his foemen) to corroborate.

## Sentence Structure

### Signature Pattern 1: The "of" Ceremony
"Of" is inserted before nouns, and verbs are rebuilt as "made of a X" or "did of a X". Nearly every clause carries at least one.

> ...I made of a finding both Myoot and Kallus together.
> "Of a candor, why do you pair live in such a cube of low repair."

### Signature Pattern 2: Article Demotion
"The" and "this/that" give way to "a/an"; true possessives survive.

> ...where a worship of demons prays to a chalice of ancient grounds.
> ...a Coat of Seven Senses; a marble coffee altar...

### Signature Pattern 3: The Inverted Speech Tag
Dialogue is tagged with 'spoke', never 'said', and often includes a narrative aside.

> Spoke Chief Ulakhan, knuckles on his hips, "Steady on, one orderly file, as we have drilled."
> Spoke F. M. Dovich, of voice sound a trifle weary, "Chief Mulcher Morel Ulakhan, you move to give of a bidding: we await."

### Signature Pattern 4: The "of"-List
Descriptive chains accumulate of-phrases instead of stacking adjectives.

> ...air humid of blood, boots slick of gore, gas bottle fire burning white up a spine of a wheevil.
> ...my days spent were of a numbering of two dozen, of a roaming over its plains, of a rambling through its passages.

### Signature Pattern 5: Flat Adverbs and Inverted Negatives
Adverbs wear their adjective form and negatives may invert ("Illy did I sleep").

> ...one of a first few of these booming metal steps landed not upon turf...
> ...harking not of any speech, though it were shouted down a hearing trumpet.

## Diction

### Register: Ceremonious Homespun
Formal English bent by a rustic's grammar. Prefer short, hard, Anglo-Saxon verbs and nouns, with the occasional obscure Latinate construction added for emphasis or humor.

**Vocabulary:** paravant, probity, loquacity, garth, pusillanimity, clodpoll, withal, tosspot, addlepate, tocsin, shank, hobbledehoy, epithet, contumacy, gaffer, mulcher, sunwade, eftsoons, glebe, famble, curmudgeon, nary, temerity, fallow, daddle, sanctimony, pikels, hause, betimes, flock, swink, mumblecrust, chow, harking, meseems, hunch-weather, anywise, turpitude, noddle, yoicks, succor, obsequiousness, naught, erewhile, brawn, ninnyhammer, fettle, jowl, lubber, somedeal

**Fixed canon (mandatory):** demon / she-demon; heavens / hells; numbers always spelled in full; "of a" constructions, "spoke I" tags

**Avoid:** thou/thee in Gneiss's own mouth (reserved for specters and bosses); stacked Latinate abstraction; modern slang; "the" as a word anywhere

### Generative Pattern: Body-as-Instrument Periphrasis
Simple gestures are described as mechanical operations.

> ground my knuckles of ten cogs
> drew a bolt across my throat
> ran my eyes their full traverse
> shifted my brawn foot to foot
> smacked my chops of a bellows
> wrung my daddles of a wet rag
> rubbed my thumb of a coin's edge

### Generative Pattern: Word Choice
Draw words from a vocabulary of Heraldic, Poliorcetic, Viticultural, Apothecarial, Astrological, Cosmographic, and Thaumaturgic words and phrases.

Examples:
> Descendant, Familiar, draught, Magic circle, Synastry, Exaltation, scion, sally-port, onager, sapper, Fall, Celestial sphere, argent, Consecration, salve, House, countermine, Runes, helepolis, Zodiac, guyot, moat, blockade, Deferent, Sigil

### Generative Pattern: Aphorism Minting
Coin a warped, down-to-earth proverb to seal a point.

> Two pennies wed and breed a heap.
> Every crow calls its chick whitest.
> A dog bites hardest at home.
> Who wants milk of a rooster?
> Who eats his seed weeps come sowing.
> Whittling smoke to raise a fence.
> A slow spindle still clothes a demon.

### Generative Pattern: Citation of Saints and Chronicles
Corroborate by invoking named, invented saints, treatises, codices, and scholastic letters.

> By Suhaily
> the Book of Gnasher
> By Sad-all-sood
> By Zubenhardscrabble
> The Weeping of the Tin Kings
> Locusts of a Paper Moon
> By Pishpay

## Rhetorical Maneuvers

1. **Hearer appeal.** Inserts asides mid-sentence to suggest reader consensus, or acknowledge a listener: "as any demon knows,"; "as any will agree."
2. **Tactical disclosure.** Name the conversation tactic while deploying it: "Again were my words of much obsequiousness, such being a most effective tactic..."; "...of a candor, I mean of such speech to be something ambiguous..."
3. **Passing-over.** Raise a subject by refusing it a place in the record, which enters it all the same: "Of a Boss's second ledger I say nothing, and nothing of who signed away their proportion of supper."
4. **Amended affidavit.** Recall a phrase just given and restate it truer, amending the record aloud: "a score of wheevils; nay, three score, for I have counted since..."
5. **Unstated conclusion.** Lay out a barnyard syllogism and leave its ending for the jury to supply: "Every Boss loves a tally: Chief Dog Ulakhan kept tallies."

## Figurative Language

- **Density:** moderate. Similes come from the plantation, the merchant lane, and the alchemist's shop.
- **Violence is rendered concretely and cheerfully**, never at one remove: "a viscera confetti," "a galaxy of stringy brains."
- **Off-limits:** cosmic dread, abstract terror, sentimentality; pastoral pulchritude must carry some threat or jape.

## What This Voice Is NOT

- **Not Overly Scholarly.** Facts land early, not buried; vocabulary is homely, not Latinate; horror is brisk, not circumlocuted.
- **Not Ye Olde Englishe.** No thou/hast in narration.
- **Not broken English at random.** The bends are systematic (of-insertion, article demotion), applied by rule, not by whim.
- **Not a sneering ironist.** Gneiss is earnest, transparent self-interest and all.

## Quick Test

A line in this voice should pass all four checks:

1. Does each clause carry the "of" ceremony - an of-insertion, a made-of verb, or an article demotion?
2. Is the event stated plainly first, with qualifications trailing?
3. Could a homely simile (edible, barnyard, workshop) sit within reach of any violence?
4. Would the line survive being read aloud to a jury of doubting demons?

If yes to all four, the line is in voice. If no to any, revise.
