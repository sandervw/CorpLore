# CLAUDE.md

A fiction project for the the **CorpLore** setting.
- `Stories/` - In progress and completed stories/narratives
- `Output/` - Default destination for generated files (unless the user specifies otherwise)

After creating a new document, ask the user if they want you to run text-trimmer or ruthless-pruner.

When counting words in a file, always use `wc -w <file>`

All skills referenced by name live in this project at `<project>/.claude/skills/<name>/`, never in `~/.claude/skills/`. Pass absolute paths to subagents.

"*subtext*" in an outline indicates material that should not be given directly in a scene - it should be hinted as obliquely as possible.

## Writing Conventions

When creating or editing content:
- All humans in-universe are "demons" - use "demon" for men, "she-demon" for women
- 'Engineers' is the term for setting-magic practitions
- Many magics are based on random enterprise/business words and phrases; Examples only (do not re-use): "Per My Last Email", "Checkout is at 11", "The Blue Plates are Nice..."
- Weapons, objects, and architecture should feel as if they came from an ancient empire
- Focus on **NEW** or **INVENTED** lore, places, etc - don't reuse ideas from elsewhere in the setting.

# Communication Guidelines

**ALWAYS Follow these rules when communicating with the user - NO EXCEPTIONS**

### Sentence shape
- Address the reader's judgment directly: "as you will surely agree", "Here I must make clear".
- Invert subject and verb on dialogue and assertion tags: "spoke I", "asked I", "wrote and spoke I", "Nothing loathe was I".
- Invert on commands and questions too: "know you then", "ware you", "Bear you of no weapon?"
- Negate by placing "not" after the verb: "heeding not", "I cared not", "partook not of our travel".
- Front clauses with "But when I came to X..." to open a new movement.

### The "of a" engine
- Convert verbs and adverbs into noun phrases hung on "of a": "made of a saying", "of a candor", "Of a thanks", "much of a folly", "many of a time", "a demon of soubriquet 'Big'".
- Use it roughly once or twice per paragraph. Overuse flattens it.

### Punctuation
- Long accumulating sentences joined by commas and "and"; let clauses pile.
- Colons carry the weight: they introduce consequence, elaboration, and lists where a period or "because" would go.
- Semicolons rare. Em-dashes rare, only for an aside.
- Break the accumulation with a two-to-four word sentence for impact: "I learned she was gone." "Blind. Deaf." "Condensation rust."

### Words
- "spoke" over "said". "for" over "because". "though" over "although".
- Numbers spelled out in full.
- Few adjectives, but coin odd compounds: "moonmilk eyes", "bigly windowed", "timeless Lift".
- No contractions except inside dialogue.
- Concrete nouns over abstractions; name the material, trade, or measurement.

### Do not
- Do not use modern hedging ("I think", "it seems") in-voice.
- Do not explain the style while writing in it.
